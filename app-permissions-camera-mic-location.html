How to Control Which Apps Can Use Your Camera, Microphone and Location on Your Samsung Galaxy

Bilal is 72 years old. He likes simple games on his phone. Last week, he installed a new game, a game where you match coloured stones. It looked harmless. It asked him one question when he opened it: "Allow this app to use your location?" Bilal stopped. Why would a stone-matching game need to know where he is? He lives in Lahore. The game does not need to know that. It does not need to know where he walks, or where he shops. He tapped "Don't allow", and the game still worked. It worked perfectly well, without knowing where he was.

A week later, Bilal installed a torch app, an app that turns the phone's light on. It asked for his location too. And it asked for his contacts, the list of people he knows. A torch needs none of those things. A torch needs only the light. Bilal deleted the app. He was right to be careful. Some apps ask for far more than they need, and most people tap "Allow" without reading, because the question appears suddenly, and "Allow" is the easy answer.

This guide teaches you how to stay in control. On your Samsung Galaxy phone, you decide which apps can use your camera, your microphone, and your location. You can check what each app is allowed to do, and you can change it at any time. You do not need to understand computers. You only need to know where the switches are, and what the choices mean.

Permissions are like keys to rooms

Think of your phone as a house. Each part of the phone is a room. The camera is one room. The microphone is another room. Your location is another room. Your contacts, the list of people you know, is another room. Your photos are another room. An app is a visitor. When an app wants to enter a room, it must ask you for the key. That question, "Allow this app to use your location?", is the app asking for the key to the location room.

You are the one who holds the keys. You can give an app a key, or you can refuse. And you can take a key back at any time. Giving an app the camera key does not give it the microphone key. Each room has its own key, and each app must ask for each one separately. That is why you see several questions, one after another, when you first open a new app. The app is asking for several keys, one at a time.

Some apps need their keys. A camera app needs the camera key, or it cannot take a photo. A map app needs the location key, or it cannot show you where you are. WhatsApp needs the microphone key, or the person you call cannot hear you. Those are fair requests. But a stone-matching game does not need the location key. A torch app does not need your contacts. When an app asks for a key it does not need, you can say no, and the app will usually still work.

The four choices your phone gives you

When an app asks for a permission, your phone usually gives you three or four choices. They sound similar, but they mean different things. Here they are, in plain words.

  1. Allow all the time. The app can use this permission whenever it wants, even when you are not using the app. Only a few apps need it, such as a map app that guides you while you drive. For most apps, this choice is too wide.
  2. Allow only while using the app. The app can use this permission only while you have it open on your screen. As soon as you leave the app, the permission stops. This is the safest useful choice for most apps. If you are not sure which choice to pick, pick this one.
  3. Ask every time. The phone will ask you again, every time the app wants to use the permission. This is useful for an app you do not trust, or for a permission you only want to give sometimes. It is a little annoying, because the question keeps coming back, but it gives you the most control.
  4. Don't allow. The app cannot use this permission at all. It cannot see your location, or hear your microphone, or open your camera. Choose this when the app clearly does not need the permission. A game does not need your location. A torch app does not need your contacts. If the app still works after you choose this, you made the right choice.

For location, there is one more choice on many phones: precise location or approximate location. Precise location means the app knows exactly where you are, down to your street. Approximate location means the app knows only roughly where you are, perhaps your town, or your part of the city. A weather app works well with approximate location. It only needs to know your town to tell you the weather. A map app needs precise location, or it cannot show you which street you are on. When you have the choice, give the wider answer only when the app truly needs it.

Check what one app can do, step by step

Before you change anything, look at what an app is already allowed to do. Pick one app that worries you, and follow these steps.

  1. Find the app on your home screen, or in the list of all your apps. Press and hold the app's picture with your finger, and keep holding it for two seconds. A small menu should appear, with the name of the app at the top, and a few choices.
  2. Look for a small letter "i" in a circle, or the words "App info". It is usually at the top of the small menu. Tap it once. A new screen should open, with the name of the app, and a list of things about it, such as how much space it uses, and how much battery it uses.
  3. Look for the word "Permissions" in the list, and tap it. You should now see a list of all the things this app is allowed to do, and all the things it is not allowed to do. The list may show "Camera", "Microphone", "Location", "Contacts", "Photos", and more. Next to each one, it should say whether the app is allowed, or not allowed, or allowed only while using the app.
  4. Read the list slowly. Does anything surprise you? A game that is allowed to know your location should surprise you. A torch app that is allowed to read your contacts should surprise you. A weather app that is allowed to use your microphone should surprise you. If something surprises you, that is the permission to change. The next part of this guide shows you how.
  5. If you cannot find "Permissions" in the App info screen, there is another way. Open Settings, and use the search box at the top of Settings to search for "Permission manager". Tap it. You should see a list of all permissions, such as Camera, Microphone, and Location. Tap one, and you will see every app that is allowed to use it, and every app that is not.

Do not worry about changing something by accident in this step. Looking is safe. You have only looked at a list. Nothing changes until you tap a permission and choose a new answer. Take your time, and look at two apps, so you get a feel for what is normal. A camera app with camera permission is normal. A map app with location permission is normal. A game with location permission is not normal, and it is worth changing.

Change a permission: allow, ask, or stop

Now change the permission that surprised you. The steps are the same as above, with one more tap at the end.

  1. Follow the steps above, until you are on the Permissions list for the app. Tap the permission you want to change. For example, tap "Location". A new screen should open, with the four choices: "Allow all the time", "Allow only while using the app", "Ask every time", and "Don't allow". On some phones, the choices are a little shorter, but the meaning is the same.
  2. Choose the answer that fits. For a game that asked for your location, choose "Don't allow". The game does not need to know where you are. For a weather app, choose "Allow only while using the app", and if it asks, choose approximate location, not precise location. For a map app, choose "Allow only while using the app", with precise location, because a map cannot guide you without knowing exactly where you are.
  3. Go back, and check the list again. The permission you changed should now show your new answer. If it says "Don't allow" next to Location for the game, you have done it. The game can no longer see where you are. If the game still works, and it should, you have lost nothing, and you have taken back control.
  4. If you change your mind later, you can change it back. Permissions are not permanent. Open the same screen again, tap the same permission, and choose a different answer. Your phone will remember your latest choice, and forget the old one. There is no penalty for changing your mind.

Two more things to know. First, the camera and the microphone have a small safety sign. When an app is using your camera or your microphone, a small green dot should appear at the top right of your screen. If you see a green dot, and you are not using the camera or making a call, swipe down from the top of the screen, and the phone should tell you which app it is. Second, some permissions are needed for the phone itself to work. The phone app needs the microphone, or you cannot make calls. The camera app needs the camera.

See which apps used your camera or microphone recently

Your phone keeps a short record of which apps used your camera, your microphone, and your location recently. This is a useful check. It is like looking at a guest book, to see who visited which room, and when.

  1. Open Settings, and use the search box at the top of Settings to search for "Privacy". Tap the result that says "Privacy", or "Security and privacy". The exact words may be a little different on your phone. Look for the word "Privacy".
  2. Look for a line that says "Permission manager", or "Privacy dashboard", or "See which apps used permissions". Tap it. You should see a list, or a chart, showing which apps used the camera, the microphone, or the location in the last day, and at what time.
  3. Read the list slowly. Does anything surprise you? A game that used your location at midnight should surprise you. An app you have not opened in a week, that used your microphone yesterday, should surprise you. If something surprises you, open that app's Permissions, as you learned above, and change it to "Don't allow", or to "Ask every time".
  4. If the list is empty, or if it shows only the apps you expect, that is good news. It means nothing strange has happened. Check this list once a month, or whenever something feels wrong. It takes two minutes, and it tells you a lot.

If you cannot find this list, do not worry. Not every phone shows it in the same place, and some older versions show less detail. The important part is the Permissions screen for each app, which every Galaxy phone has.

Practise with Bilal: stop a game knowing where you are

Let us practise with Bilal and his stone-matching game. Bilal opens the game, and it asks for his location. He does not want the game to know where he is. He presses and holds the game's picture on his home screen, and he taps "App info". He taps "Permissions". He sees that Location says "Allow only while using the app". He taps "Location", and he chooses "Don't allow". He goes back, and the list now says "Don't allow" next to Location. He opens the game again. It works. The stones still match. The game does not know where he is, and it does not need to know. Bilal has lost nothing, and he has taken back control.

Now try the same practice yourself, with one app that worries you. Pick the app. Press and hold its picture. Tap "App info". Tap "Permissions". Read the list. Change one permission that surprises you. Then open the app, and check that it still works. Do this once, slowly, and you will understand permissions better than any amount of reading.

If an app stops working after you change a permission

Sometimes, after you change a permission, an app stops working properly. Do not panic. This is normal, and it is easy to fix. Check these things, in order.

  1. The app truly needs the permission. A map app without location cannot show you where you are. A camera app without camera permission cannot take a photo. WhatsApp without microphone permission cannot let the other person hear you. If the app stopped working, and it is an app that truly needs that permission, open its Permissions again, and choose "Allow only while using the app". That is the safe middle answer. The app works, and it can only use the permission while you have it open.
  2. The app needs to be closed and opened again. Some apps do not notice a change until they are closed and opened again. Close the app completely, by swiping it away from the list of recent apps, and then open it again. It should now work with the new permission.
  3. The app is asking again, and you keep saying no. Some apps ask for the same permission every time you open them. If you keep saying no, and the app keeps asking, that is the app telling you it wants that permission. Read the app's question again. If the app truly needs it, choose "Allow only while using the app". If it clearly does not need it, keep saying no, and consider deleting the app. An app that nags you for a permission it does not need is not a good app.
  4. The phone itself needs the permission. If you turned off a permission for a phone app, such as the Phone app or the Camera app, the phone may not work properly. Open the Permissions for that app again, and turn the permission back on. The phone's own apps are safe to trust with the permissions they need to do their job.

If none of these four reasons fits, ask a family member to look at the Permissions screen with you. A second pair of eyes often spots a different answer in seconds. Do not download an app that claims to manage your permissions for you. The real switches are built into the phone, and they are reached through App info and Permission manager. An app that promises to do it for you is not needed, and it may be a scam.

Common mistakes

  • Tapping "Allow" without reading the question. The question appears suddenly, and "Allow" is the easy answer. Slow down. Read the question. Ask yourself: does this app need this permission to do its job?
  • Choosing "Allow all the time" for every app. "Allow all the time" is the widest choice. It lets the app use the permission even when you are not using it. For most apps, "Allow only while using the app" is enough. Reserve "Allow all the time" for the few apps that truly need it, such as a map app while you drive.
  • Thinking that "Don't allow" will break the phone. It will not. Changing a permission for one app affects only that app. The phone itself keeps working. And you can always change it back. Permissions are switches, not one-way doors.
  • Forgetting to check old apps. The apps you installed months ago may have permissions you gave them without thinking. Once a month, look at the Permissions for two or three old apps. You may be surprised at what they are allowed to do.
  • Ignoring the small green dot. When an app uses your camera or microphone, a small green dot should appear at the top right of your screen. If you see it, and you are not using the camera or making a call, swipe down and check which app it is. Do not ignore it.
  • Believing an app that promises to protect your privacy. Some apps claim to manage your permissions, or to block other apps from watching you. You do not need them. The real switches are built into the phone. An app that promises to protect you may itself be asking for permissions it does not need.
  • Giving a weather app your precise location. A weather app only needs to know your town. Choose approximate location, not precise location, when the phone offers the choice. Your exact street is none of a weather app's business.

Frequently asked questions

Will an app stop working if I choose "Don't allow"?

Usually, no. Most apps work fine without permissions they do not need. A game works without your location. A torch app works without your contacts. If an app does stop working, it is telling you that it truly needs that permission. Open its Permissions again, and choose "Allow only while using the app". That is the safe middle answer.

How do I know if an app is using my camera right now?

Look for a small green dot at the top right of your screen. When an app is using your camera or your microphone, the phone shows a small green dot there. If you see it, and you are not using the camera or making a call, swipe down from the top of the screen. The phone should tell you which app is using it.

Can an app track where I go, even when I am not using it?

Only if you chose "Allow all the time" for its location permission. That choice lets the app see your location even when you are not using it. Most apps do not need that choice. Open the app's Permissions, tap "Location", and change it to "Allow only while using the app", or to "Don't allow".

Should I let every app use my location?

No. Only the apps that truly need it. A map app needs it. A weather app needs only your town, not your exact street. A taxi app needs it while you book a ride. A game, a torch app, and a photo-editing app do not need it at all. When in doubt, choose "Don't allow", and see if the app still works.

What is the difference between precise and approximate location?

Precise location means the app knows exactly where you are, down to your street. Approximate location means the app knows only roughly where you are, perhaps your town, or your part of the city. A weather app works well with approximate location. A map app needs precise location, or it cannot show you which street you are on. When the phone offers the choice, give the wider answer only when the app truly needs it.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words may look a little different on your phone. That is normal. Samsung makes many Galaxy models. Some are A-series phones. Some are S-series phones. They do not all show the exact same words. Your phone company, called a carrier, can also change some words. Your country or region can change words too. And your One UI version matters. In October 2026, some phones use One UI 8, some use One UI 8.5, and some use One UI 9, depending on the model and the region. No one version is "the" version for every phone. That is why this guide tells you to look for the words "Permissions" and "Permission manager", and to use the search box at the top of Settings and type "Permission manager" if you cannot find them.

If you cannot find "App info" when you press and hold an app's picture, check two things. First, check that you pressed and held for two full seconds. A quick tap only opens the app. Press, hold, and wait for the small menu to appear. Second, check that you are pressing the app's picture on the home screen, or in the list of all apps, not inside the app itself. If the small menu still does not appear, open Settings, use the search box at the top to search for "Apps", tap the result, find the app in the list, and tap it.
