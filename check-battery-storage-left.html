How to Check How Much Battery and Storage You Have Left on Your Galaxy

Harold is seventy-three. He was on a day trip with his walking group last month. At lunch he tried to take a photo of the group by a lake. His phone showed a message. He could not take the photo. The phone said the storage was full. Harold did not know what storage meant. He thought the phone was broken. He missed the group photo, and he felt embarrassed in front of his friends.

Two numbers help you avoid that surprise. The first is the battery percentage. It tells you how much charge is left. The second is the storage number. It tells you how much space is left for photos, videos, and apps. Both numbers are easy to read once you know where to look. This guide shows you where to find them, what the numbers mean in plain words, and what to do when a number gets low. It does not make any claim about battery health. It only shows you how to read the numbers your phone already shows.

Checking these numbers takes less than a minute. Do it once a week, perhaps on Sunday morning with a cup of tea. You will spot a low battery before you leave the house. You will spot low storage before a birthday or a trip. A quick check saves worry later. Go slowly and write the numbers down the first time, so you can compare them next week.

What battery and storage mean in plain words

Think of the battery as a water jug. A full jug is one hundred percent. As you use the phone, the jug empties. At fifty percent, half the charge is left. At twenty percent, only a little is left, and you should charge soon. At five percent, the phone may switch off at any time. The percentage is shown as a number with a small battery picture. Bigger is better. There is no need to memorise anything else about the battery for this guide.

Think of storage as a cupboard with shelves. Photos, videos, apps, and messages take up shelf space. When the cupboard is nearly full, there is no room for new photos. Your phone shows storage as used space and free space, measured in gigabytes, written as GB. For example, it may say you have used 80 GB out of 128 GB. That means 48 GB is still free. Free space is the room you have left. Used space is the room already taken. When free space gets very small, the phone may warn you.

Battery and storage are separate. A phone can have a full battery and full storage at the same time. A phone can have low battery and plenty of storage. Check both numbers. They answer different questions. Battery answers, How long can I keep using the phone before I need to charge? Storage answers, Can I take more photos and add more apps?

How to read the battery percentage

The fastest way to see the battery is at the top of the screen. Look at the top right corner. You will see a small battery icon. On many phones the percentage number sits beside the icon, such as 72 percent. On some phones the number is hidden until you swipe down. Both views show the same thing.

  1. Look at the top of your home screen. Find the small battery icon in the top right corner. If you see a number beside it, that is your battery percentage. Note the number. That is all you need for a quick check.
  2. If you see no number, swipe down from the top of the screen with one finger. A panel opens. Look for the battery icon and number in that panel. The number is larger and easier to read there.
  3. Swipe down with two fingers for the full Quick settings panel if you want an even clearer view. The battery percentage is shown near the top of that panel on many phones. Note the number, then swipe up to close the panel.
  4. Compare the number with these simple guides. Above 50 percent means plenty of charge for most of the day. Between 20 and 50 percent means charge when you can, but there is no rush. Below 20 percent means plug in soon, especially if you are going out. Below 10 percent means plug in now if you can.

If your phone does not show the percentage beside the icon, you can look for a setting that turns the number on. The wording for that setting can differ by phone, so do not guess a path from memory. Open Settings, use the search box at the top, and type the word battery. Look for a choice about showing the battery percentage. Turn it on if you see it. If your phone does not offer that choice, the swipe down panel still shows the number, and that is enough.

How to check battery and storage in Device care

Device care is the part of Settings that gathers battery and storage in one place. The verified path is Settings, then Device care, then Battery or Storage. Some phones call this section Battery and device care instead of Device care. The name can differ a little, but the idea is the same. Inside, you will see a battery section and a storage section. Tap each one to read its numbers.

  1. Open Settings. Tap the gear icon on your home screen or in your apps list. The Settings list opens.
  2. Scroll down and tap Device care. If your phone says Battery and device care, tap that instead. It is the same place. A new screen opens with a few boxes, often including Battery, Storage, Memory, and Security.
  3. Tap Battery. The battery screen opens. It shows the percentage left, in large numbers. It may also show how long the battery is expected to last, based on how you have used the phone. Read the percentage only for this guide. Do not worry about the other details on this screen.
  4. Go back one step. Tap the back arrow at the top left, or swipe back, to return to the Device care screen.
  5. Tap Storage. The storage screen opens. It shows a bar or a circle. The bar shows used space in one colour and free space in another. Below the bar you will see numbers, such as total space and free space in GB. Read the free space number. That is the room you have left.
  6. Look at the list below the numbers if your phone shows one. It may break the used space into groups, such as Apps, Images, Videos, and Audio. This list only tells you what kind of files take up room. It does not tell you to delete anything. Read it to understand where the space has gone.
  7. Write down both numbers. Write the battery percentage and the free storage number on a piece of paper, with the date. For example, write, Sunday, battery 68 percent, storage free 32 GB. Next week, check again and compare. A steady fall in free space tells you photos and videos are filling the cupboard.

Device care may also show a button that says Optimise or Optimise now. You do not need to tap it for this guide. The button is meant to tidy some temporary files and settings. It is not needed to read the numbers. If you tap it by mistake, it will not delete your photos. Still, for a first check, read the numbers and leave the other buttons alone. Go back to the home screen when you have your two numbers.

What to do when a number is low

A low battery is simple to fix. Plug the phone in to charge. Use the charger that came with the phone, or a charger a family member has checked for you. Plug it in at home, in a place where you can see it. Leave it to charge. The percentage will climb. You can still use the phone while it charges, but it will charge faster if you leave the screen off. If you must go out with a low battery, take the charger and a cable with you, or plan a short outing.

Low storage needs more care. Do not delete photos in a hurry. First, look at the storage list in Device care. See which group is largest. Often videos take the most room. A few long videos can fill many gigabytes. If you have videos you have already shared with family, or backed up elsewhere, those are the safest to review first. Ask a family member to help you check what is backed up before you delete anything. Never delete photos of family as a first step. Start by asking, Do I have copies of these photos somewhere else, such as a family member phone or a computer?

You can also free a little space by deleting apps you never use. Open the apps list, look for an app you do not recognise or have not opened in months, and ask a family member if it is safe to remove. Do not remove apps with names you do not understand without asking. Some apps are part of the phone system. Removing a system app can cause confusion. When in doubt, leave the app and ask for help. Storage can wait a day. Deleting the wrong thing cannot always be undone.

Try it slowly: Harold checks before the next trip

Harold practised the check on a quiet morning at home. He looked at the top right of his home screen. The battery showed 81 percent. That was plenty. He opened Settings, tapped Device care, and tapped Storage. The screen showed he had used most of his space. Only 4 GB was free. Now he understood the lake photo problem. The cupboard was nearly full. He did not delete anything on his own. He called his son, Peter, that evening.

Peter sat with Harold the next day. They looked at the storage list together. Videos took the most room. Peter checked that the family videos were copied to his own phone and to a computer at home. Then, together, they deleted three long videos Harold had already shared and no longer needed on the phone. Free space rose from 4 GB to 18 GB. Harold wrote the new numbers down. He said, Now I check every Sunday. I will not miss another group photo. Harold also set a habit. He plugs the phone in each night, so the battery starts each morning near full.

Try the same routine. Check on Sunday. Write the two numbers down. If free space falls below 10 GB, tell a family member before the next trip or family event. Ask for help reviewing videos and apps. Do not rush to delete. A calm check once a week keeps both numbers in safe territory.

Common mistakes

These mistakes lead to surprises. A weekly check avoids most of them.

  • Only looking at the battery icon and guessing the charge. A half full icon can mean 40 percent or 60 percent. Read the number. The number tells you the truth. Swipe down if the number is not beside the icon.
  • Thinking a storage warning means the phone is broken. A full storage warning means the cupboard is full, not that the phone is faulty. Check Device care, read the free space number, and ask for help reviewing files. The phone itself is usually fine.
  • Deleting precious photos first to free space. Photos are hard to get back. Check videos and unused apps first, and check for copies elsewhere. Ask a family member before you delete any photo of family, a holiday, or a special event.
  • Leaving the phone uncharged until it switches off. A phone that switches off can miss calls and alarms. Charge at 20 percent or below when you are at home. Plug in each night if that suits your routine. A full morning battery removes a daily worry.
  • Reading the total storage number as the free space number. The total is the size of the whole cupboard. The free space is the empty room left. Always look for the word free. For example, 128 GB total with 12 GB free means only 12 GB is left, not 128 GB.
  • Trusting one check and never checking again. Battery and storage change every week as you take photos and use apps. One check tells you today only. Check weekly, write the numbers down, and compare. The habit matters more than any single number.

Frequently asked questions

What battery percentage is safe to leave the house with?

Above 50 percent is comfortable for a normal outing. Between 20 and 50 percent is fine for a short trip if you do not use the phone heavily. Below 20 percent, charge before you go out if you can, or take a charger with you. If you plan to take many photos or use maps, start with a higher charge, as the camera and screen use more power.

How much free storage do I need to take photos?

There is no single exact number, because photos and videos vary in size. As a simple guide, keep at least 5 GB free for everyday photos. For a day trip with many photos and videos, aim for at least 10 GB free before you leave. If free space is below 5 GB, ask a family member to help you review videos and apps before the trip. Do not wait until the phone warns you during the event.

Does the battery percentage tell me if my battery is healthy?

No. The percentage only tells you how much charge is left right now. It does not tell you whether the battery holds charge as well as it did when the phone was new. This guide only shows you how to read the charge left. If your phone runs out very quickly even after a full charge, tell a family member or take it to a reputable shop for advice. Do not try to open the phone yourself.

What do the storage groups mean?

The groups tell you what kind of files use space. Apps means the apps themselves and their data. Images means photos. Videos means video files. Audio means music and sound files. Documents and other files may appear as a separate group. The groups help you see where space has gone. They do not mean you must delete a whole group. Review one group at a time, with help if needed.

Will checking Device care delete anything?

No, simply opening Device care and reading the Battery and Storage screens deletes nothing. Reading is safe. Only tapping a delete, clear, or uninstall button removes something. For this guide, read the numbers, write them down, and go back. Leave delete buttons alone unless a family member is with you and agrees.

Why does my free space fall even when I take no photos?

Apps and messages can grow over time. Apps store temporary files. Messaging apps store photos and videos sent by others. Software updates can also take space. That is why a weekly check helps. A slow fall is normal. A sudden large fall is worth asking a family member to look at with you, to see which group grew.

If the words on your screen look different

Device care is not named the same way on every Galaxy phone. Some phones say Device care. Others say Battery and device care. Both lead to the same place. Inside, the words Battery and Storage should be easy to spot. On an A series phone the boxes may sit in a different order from an S series phone. The numbers still mean the same thing. The battery percentage is the charge left. The free space number is the room left for new files.

Menu words can also shift with your region, your phone company, and your One UI version. In October 2026, One UI 8, One UI 8.5, and One UI 9 are all in use, so two phones bought in different months may show slightly different labels. If you cannot find Device care, open Settings and use the search box at the top. Type Device care. Tap the result your phone shows. If the search shows Battery and device care instead, tap that. If you still cannot find it, look for the word Battery in Settings search, and follow the result. The battery screen and the storage screen are close together, so finding one usually leads you to the other.
