How to Save a Contact and Find It Again on Your Galaxy

Fatima is 63 years old. Her granddaughter Zara has a new phone number. Zara wrote the number on a small piece of paper. She gave the paper to Fatima after Sunday dinner. Fatima put the paper in her handbag. Two days later, she wanted to call Zara. She opened her handbag. The paper was gone. It had slipped behind her purse. Fatima felt upset. She did not want to lose Zara's number. She did not want to ask Zara again and look forgetful. She wanted the number safe in her phone, where she could find it in seconds.

Saving a contact means storing a person's name and phone number together in your phone. After you save it, you do not need the piece of paper. You do not need to remember the number. You just search for the name. The phone shows you the number. You tap to call. You tap to send a message. It is like a small address book that lives inside your phone. It is private. Only you can see it when you unlock your phone. No one else will see your contacts unless you show them.

This guide will help you save a new contact. It will also help you find a contact again later. The two skills go together. Saving is only half the job. Finding is the other half. Many people save a number, then cannot find it the next day. They look in the wrong app. They spell the name a different way. They give up. You will not give up. You will learn both steps, slowly, with a clear example. You will practise with a real name and a real number you know well.

Before you start, have the number ready. Write it on a piece of paper in large, clear digits. Or ask the person to send it to you in a message, so you can copy it carefully. Check every digit. One wrong digit means you will call the wrong person. Sit down. Put your glasses on if you use them. Do not try to save a number while you are walking, cooking, or watching television. A quiet minute now will save you trouble later. If the number is long, read it out loud, digit by digit, as you type it. That helps you catch mistakes.

Save a new contact, step by step

Follow these steps in order. The exact words and icons may look a little different on your phone, so this guide tells you what to look for, not only the exact word on every model.

  1. Find the Contacts app on your phone. Look for an icon that looks like a person, or a small address book, or a list of names. The app may be called Contacts. Tap it once to open it. If you cannot find it, open the Phone app, the one you use to make calls, and look for a tab or a word that says Contacts near the bottom or the top of the screen. Tap that.
  2. Look for a button to add a new contact. It often looks like a plus sign, or a person with a plus sign, or the word Create, or the word Add. It is often near the top or the bottom of the screen. Tap it once. A blank form will open. This form is where you type the name and the number.
  3. Tap the box or line that says Name, or First name. A keyboard will appear at the bottom of the screen. Type the person's first name. For Fatima, that is Zara. Type it slowly. Check the spelling. Use the name you will remember. If you know the person by a nickname, use that nickname. For example, if you always call her Zara, save her as Zara. Do not save her as a long formal name you will never search for.
  4. Tap the box or line for the phone number. It may say Phone, or Mobile, or Phone number. Type the number, digit by digit. Read the number from your paper as you type. Do not guess a digit. If you are not sure about a digit, stop. Check the paper again. When you have typed all the digits, read the number back to yourself. Compare it with the paper, digit by digit, from left to right.
  5. If the form has a box for a second name, a family name, or a note, you can leave it empty. You do not need to fill in every box. The name and the number are enough. If you want to remember why you saved this person, you can add a short note, such as "Zara, new number, October". But a note is optional. Keep it simple for now.
  6. Look for the Save button. It is often at the top or the bottom of the form. It may say Save. Tap Save once. Wait a second. The form should close. You should see the new contact, with the name and the number. If the form does not close, look again for Save. Do not tap the back arrow before you tap Save, or your new contact may be lost.
  7. Check your work. You should now see the name you just saved in your contacts list. Tap the name once to open it. Check the number. Is every digit right? If a digit is wrong, look for an Edit button, often shaped like a small pencil, or the word Edit. Tap it. Fix the digit. Tap Save again. It is much better to fix a mistake now than to call the wrong person later.

You have saved a contact. Well done. The number is now in your phone. You do not need the piece of paper any more, but keep the paper for a few days, just in case. After a few days, when you have called the number once and it worked, you can throw the paper away. Keeping the paper for a short time is a good safety step. It is not a sign that you failed. It is a sign that you are careful.

Find a contact again, step by step

Saving is only useful if you can find the contact later. Here is how to find it.

  1. Open the Contacts app again, in the same way as before. Look for the person icon or the word Contacts. Tap it.
  2. Look for a search box at the top of the contacts list. It may say Search. Tap it. Type the first few letters of the name. For Fatima, type Z, A, R. You do not need to type the whole name. The phone will show matching names as you type.
  3. Look at the list that appears. Tap the correct name once. You will see the contact details. You will see the number. From here, you can tap to call, or tap to send a message. The buttons for call and message are usually near the name, and they look like a telephone handset and a speech bubble. Tap the one you want.
  4. If no name appears when you search, check your spelling. Did you save the person as Zara, or as Zahra? Did you save a nickname? Try typing only the first letter. Scroll slowly through the list. Look with your eyes, not only with search. Sometimes a contact is there, but under a spelling you did not expect.

If you scroll through the whole list and the name is truly not there, the contact may not have saved. That happens if Save was not tapped. Do not worry. Go back to the steps above and save it again. This time, watch for the Save button. Wait until the form closes. Then search for the name at once, to prove to yourself that it saved. That quick check takes ten seconds. It gives you confidence.

Try it slowly: Fatima saves Zara, then saves the clinic

Let us practise with Fatima. Fatima has Zara's new number on paper. She sits at her kitchen table. She opens Contacts. She taps the plus sign to add a new contact. She types Zara in the Name box. She types the number slowly, reading it out loud. She taps Save. She searches for Zara. Zara appears. Fatima taps the name. The number is correct. She feels calm. The paper can now stay in a drawer, as a backup.

A week later, Fatima needs to save a second number. It is the number for her doctor's clinic. The clinic gave her a card. The card says the clinic name and the number. Fatima saves it as Clinic, not as a person's name. She types Clinic in the Name box. She types the number from the card. She taps Save. She searches for Clinic. It appears. Now she has two new contacts: Zara and Clinic. Both are easy to find, because she used names she will remember.

Now try it yourself with a practice contact. Pick a person you call often, whose number you already have saved, or a number you know well. Or pick a safe practice: save the number of a family member, then search for it, then call them and tell them you were practising. Do not practise with a number you found on the internet or in a strange message. Practise with a number you trust. Save it. Find it. Call it once. That full circle, save, find, call, is the skill. Repeat it with a second contact the next day. After two or three saves, the steps will feel familiar. Your hands will remember where to tap.

Think about how you will name people. Use one clear name for each person. If you have two friends called Ahmed, save them as Ahmed Brother, or Ahmed Neighbour, or Ahmed Work. Add a word that tells you which Ahmed it is. That small extra word will save you from calling the wrong Ahmed. Do the same for other shared names. A clear name is a kind gift to your future self.

Common mistakes

  • Saving a number without a name, or with only a number as the name. Later, you will not know whose number it is. Always type a name you will remember. A number alone is almost impossible to find again.
  • Typing the number too fast and getting one digit wrong. One wrong digit can call a stranger. Type slowly. Read the number out loud. Check it digit by digit before you tap Save. Check it again after you save it.
  • Tapping the back arrow before tapping Save. The new contact is then lost. It never saves. Always look for Save. Tap Save. Wait for the form to close. Then search for the name to prove it saved.
  • Searching in the Messages app instead of the Contacts app. Messages shows only people you have already messaged. Contacts shows everyone you saved. If you cannot find a name in Messages, open Contacts and search there. That is where all saved names live.
  • Saving the same person twice, with two different spellings. You end up with Zara and Zahra, and you do not know which number is new. If you find a duplicate, open one, check the number, and delete the old one only when you are sure. Ask a family member to help if you are not sure which one to keep.
  • Using a formal name you will never search for. If you always call someone Auntie Salma, save her as Auntie Salma. Do not save her under a long name you never use. You will search for the name you say out loud. Save that name.

Frequently asked questions

Can I change a number after I save it?

Yes. Open Contacts. Find the person. Tap their name. Look for an Edit button, often a small pencil shape, or the word Edit. Tap it. Change the number. Tap Save. Check the new number by reading it back. If you are not sure, ask the person to confirm the new number in a message, and copy it carefully.

What if a person changes their number and I save the new one?

Do not save a second contact with a slightly different name. Open the old contact. Edit it. Replace the old number with the new number. Tap Save. Now there is only one contact for that person, with the new number. That keeps your list clean and avoids calling the old number by mistake.

Can I delete a contact I no longer need?

Yes. Open the contact. Look for a menu, often three small dots, and look for the word Delete, or look for a Delete button. Your phone may ask, "Are you sure?" Read that question carefully. Only confirm if you are sure. If you delete a contact by mistake, you will need the number again from the person. So delete slowly, and only when you are certain.

Will my contacts be lost if my phone breaks?

Often, contacts are backed up to your account, if backup is turned on. But do not rely on memory alone. Keep important numbers written in a small notebook at home, as a backup. Write the name and the number in clear handwriting. Keep the notebook in one safe place. That way, even if the phone is lost, the important numbers are not lost with it.

Can I add more than a phone number, like an email address?

Yes, the contact form often has boxes for email and other details. But you do not need to fill them in. For most daily use, a name and a phone number are enough. Add more details only if you want to. Keep the form simple. A simple contact you can find is better than a detailed contact you never finish saving.

Why can I not find a contact I am sure I saved?

Check three things. First, check the spelling you used. Try the first letter only. Second, check if you saved it under a nickname. Third, check if Save was tapped. If Save was not tapped, the contact never saved. Save it again, tap Save, and search for it at once. If it still does not appear, ask a trusted family member to watch you save it once. They may spot a small step you missed.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words and icons may look a little different on your phone. That is normal. Samsung makes many Galaxy models. Some are A-series phones. Some are S-series phones. They do not all show the exact same words. Your phone company, called a carrier, can also change some words. Your country or region can change words too. And your One UI version matters. In October 2026, some phones use One UI 8, some use One UI 8.5, and some use One UI 9, depending on the model and the region. No one version is "the" version for every phone.

The Contacts app may also look a little different after an update. The add button may be a plus sign, or it may say Create or Add. The Save button may be at the top on one phone and at the bottom on another. Look for the meaning, not only the exact place. Look for a plus sign to add. Look for the word Save to save. Look for a pencil shape to edit. If a word looks different in Settings, use the search box at the top of Settings and type the name of the setting. If you are inside Contacts and you cannot find a button, look slowly at each corner of the screen. Take your time. There is no penalty for looking carefully.
