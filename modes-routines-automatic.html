How to Use Modes and Routines on Your Samsung Galaxy (Phone Turns Quiet at Night and in the Car by Itself)

Fatima is 68 years old. She goes to bed at ten o'clock every night, and she puts her phone on the small table beside her bed. Then, almost every night, the same thing happens. A message arrives at half past eleven. The phone lights up. It makes a sound. Fatima wakes up. It is never urgent, but her sleep is broken, and she lies awake for a long time after. In the morning, her son tells her, "Mum, just turn on Do Not Disturb before bed." Fatima tries. She remembers on Monday. She forgets on Tuesday. Remembering one more thing every night is exactly the kind of small job that is easy to forget, because the day is long and the mind is tired by bedtime.

Her phone can remember for her. On a Samsung Galaxy phone, there is a feature called Modes and Routines. It lets the phone do a job by itself, at the right time, without being asked. At ten o'clock at night, the phone turns quiet by itself. In the morning, it goes back to normal by itself. When Fatima gets into her son's car, the phone can turn quiet by itself too, because it knows it is in the car. This guide teaches you how to set up two useful routines: a Sleep routine for bedtime, and a Driving routine for the car. It also teaches you how to check that a routine is working, how to pause it, and how to delete it if you change your mind.

First, the two words. A Mode is a ready-made state for your phone. Think of it as a mood for the phone. Sleep mode is one mood: quiet, dim, and calm. Driving mode is another mood: quiet, with the screen kept simple. A Routine is a rule you make yourself. A routine says, "When this happens, do that." When the time is ten o'clock at night, turn on Do Not Disturb. When the phone connects to the car, turn on Do Not Disturb. The "when" part is called a trigger. The "do that" part is called an action. Every routine has a trigger and an action. If you understand that one sentence, you understand Routines.

Before you start, you need two things. First, your phone, with enough battery to stay on while you work through the steps. Setting up a routine takes about ten minutes. Second, a little patience. Read the words on the screens slowly. There is no rush, and nothing in this guide will harm your phone. You can undo everything you do here.

Find Modes and Routines on your phone

Modes and Routines lives in Settings. The exact place can be different on different Galaxy phones, so this guide tells you to search for it, rather than sending you down one fixed path that may not match your phone.

  1. Open Settings. Settings is the app with a picture that looks like a small gear, or cog wheel. Find it in your apps list and tap it once.
  2. Look at the top of the Settings screen. You should see a search box, with words like "Search" or a small magnifying glass picture. Tap the search box. A keyboard should appear.
  3. Type the words "Modes and Routines". Type slowly, and watch the screen. Before you finish typing, a list of results should appear. Look for a result that says "Modes and Routines". Tap that result once.
  4. The Modes and Routines screen should open. It usually has two parts at the top: "Modes" and "Routines". Modes shows ready-made moods, such as Sleep, Driving, Exercise, Work, and Relax. Routines shows the rules you have made, and a button to make a new one.

If you cannot find Modes and Routines in the search results, check the spelling first. It is three words, "Modes and Routines". Then check that your phone is a Samsung Galaxy phone. Modes and Routines is a Samsung feature. If your phone is a Galaxy and the search still shows nothing, your phone may need a software update first. Read How to Update Your Galaxy Safely, and then try the search again. Do not download an app that claims to be Modes and Routines. The real one is built into the phone, and it is reached through Settings.

Make a Sleep routine, step by step

This is the most useful routine in this guide. It turns the phone quiet at your bedtime, and it turns the sound back on in the morning. You will make it in two parts: first the trigger (when it starts and when it ends), then the action (what the phone does). Read each step, then do it, then read the next step. Go slowly.

  1. On the Modes and Routines screen, look for the "Routines" part, and look for a button that says something like "Add routine", or a plus sign, a + mark. Tap it. A new screen should open, with two boxes: one for the trigger, and one for the action. The trigger box may say "If", and the action box may say "Then". Those are the same two parts you already know: when this happens, do that.
  2. Tap the trigger box first, the one that says "If". A list of trigger types should appear: time, place, a Bluetooth device, the battery, an app, and more. You only need one. Look for the word "Time", and tap it.
  3. The phone should show a time picker, with a start time and an end time. Set the start time to the time you usually go to bed. For Fatima, that is 10:00 PM. Set the end time to the time you usually wake up. For Fatima, that is 6:00 AM. Check whether the phone shows AM or PM, and make sure they are right. A start time of 10:00 in the morning, instead of 10:00 at night, would turn the phone quiet for the whole day.
  4. Look for the days of the week under the time. If you go to bed at the same time every night, leave all seven days selected. If your bedtime is different on some days, tap the days you want, and leave the others alone. For most people, all seven days is right.
  5. Tap the button that saves the trigger. It may say "Done", or "Save", or a tick mark at the top of the screen. You should be back on the new routine screen, and the trigger box should now say something like "10:00 PM to 6:00 AM, every day". If it says something else, tap the trigger box again and fix the time.
  6. Now tap the action box, the one that says "Then". A list of action types should appear. You want the phone to turn quiet. Look for the word "Sound" or "Do Not Disturb", and tap it. If you see both, choose "Do Not Disturb". Do Not Disturb is the phone's quiet mode: calls and messages still arrive, but they do not make a sound or light up the screen. Read How to Turn On Silent Mode and Do Not Disturb on Your Galaxy if you want to understand Do Not Disturb on its own first.
  7. On the Do Not Disturb action, make sure it is set to turn on, not to turn off. If it says "Turn off", change it to "Turn on". Then save this action, with the same "Done" or tick button.
  8. You can add a second action if you want to. A second action that helps many people is to make the screen dimmer at night. Tap the "Add action" button, look for "Brightness" or "Display", and set the brightness to a low level. This is optional. If it feels like too much for today, skip it. One action, Do Not Disturb, is already a good routine.
  9. Now save the whole routine. Look for a "Save" button, or a tick mark, at the top of the screen. The phone may ask you to give the routine a name. Type a simple name, such as "Sleep at night". A clear name helps you find the routine later.
  10. Check your new routine. You should be back on the Routines list, and your new routine should be there, with its name and its time. Next to it, there should be a switch, turned on. If the switch is off, tap it once to turn it on. A routine with its switch off will not run. This is the most common reason a routine "does not work": it was saved, but its switch was left off.

That is the Sleep routine done. Tonight, at your bedtime, the phone will turn quiet by itself. In the morning, at your wake-up time, the sound will come back by itself. For the first two or three nights, check in the morning that the routine worked. If it worked, leave it alone. It will keep working, night after night, until you change it.

Make a Driving routine, step by step

The Driving routine uses a different trigger: not a time, but a connection. When your phone connects to your car's Bluetooth, the phone knows it is in the car, and it turns quiet by itself. When you leave the car and the Bluetooth connection ends, the phone goes back to normal by itself.

Before you make this routine, you need to know one thing: the phone can only tell it is in the car if it connects to the car's Bluetooth. If your car has no Bluetooth, or if the phone has never been connected to the car, this routine cannot work. That is not a fault in your phone. It is how the trigger works: no Bluetooth connection, no trigger, no routine.

  1. On the Modes and Routines screen, go to the "Routines" part, and tap the button to add a routine, the same "Add routine" or plus sign you used before.
  2. Tap the trigger box, the one that says "If". This time, do not choose Time. Look for the word "Bluetooth" or "Bluetooth device", and tap it. The phone may show a list of Bluetooth devices it knows. Look for the name of your car. It may be the name of the car, or a name like "Car Audio". Tap the name of your car.
  3. The phone should show when the trigger happens: when the phone connects to that Bluetooth device. Make sure it says "When connected", not "When disconnected". If it says the wrong one, change it. Then save the trigger, with "Done" or the tick button.
  4. Now tap the action box, the one that says "Then". Choose "Do Not Disturb" again, set to turn on, just as you did for the Sleep routine. Save the action.
  5. Save the whole routine, with "Save" or the tick button. Give it a simple name, such as "In the car". Check the Routines list: the new routine should be there, with its switch turned on. If the switch is off, tap it once to turn it on.
  6. Test it the next time you get into the car. When the phone connects to the car's Bluetooth, Do Not Disturb should turn on by itself. You can check by looking at the small moon symbol at the top of the screen, which means Do Not Disturb is on. When you leave the car, the moon symbol should go away by itself, and the sound should come back.

A plain warning, because this matters. A Driving routine does not make driving safe by itself. It only turns the phone quiet. The driver must still watch the road, not the phone. If you are the driver, do not look at the phone, do not answer messages, and do not try to change a routine while driving. Set the routine up at home, before you travel, and leave it alone in the car.

Try it slowly: one night with the Sleep routine

Let us practise with Fatima and one night. Fatima set up her Sleep routine on a Sunday afternoon. Her trigger is 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM, every day. Her action is Do Not Disturb, turned on. That evening, she does nothing different. She puts the phone on the table beside her bed, as usual. At ten o'clock, she looks at the phone. The small moon symbol is at the top of the screen. The phone has turned quiet by itself. At half past eleven, a group message arrives. The phone stays dark. It makes no sound. Fatima sleeps. At six o'clock in the morning, the moon symbol is gone. The sound is back. Her alarm still works, because an alarm is allowed to sound even when Do Not Disturb is on. If your routine works on the first night, leave it alone. It will keep working, night after night, until you change it. Also check that your alarm is set. Read How to Set an Alarm on Your Galaxy if you are unsure.

Check, pause, and delete a routine

A routine is not a promise you cannot change. You can check it, pause it, and delete it, at any time.

  1. Open Modes and Routines again, through Settings search, in the same way as before. Go to the "Routines" part of the screen.
  2. To check a routine, tap its name. The routine should open, showing its trigger and its actions. Read them slowly. Does the trigger say the time you wanted? Does the action say Do Not Disturb, turned on? If something is wrong, tap the part that is wrong, fix it, and save.
  3. To pause a routine for a while, without deleting it, find the switch next to its name on the Routines list, and tap it once to turn it off. The routine will stay on the list, but it will not run. This is useful if you are staying up late for a family event. Turn the switch off in the evening, and turn it back on the next day.
  4. To delete a routine for good, tap its name to open it, then look for a menu: three small dots, or the word "More", in a corner of the screen. Tap it, and look for the word "Delete". Tap "Delete", and confirm if the phone asks you to. The routine will disappear from the list. This cannot be undone, but that is fine: a deleted routine is easy to make again, and this guide will still be here.
  5. If you have two routines and one of them is not running, check that both switches are on. Each routine has its own switch. Turning on one routine does not turn on the other.

If a routine does not run, check these things first

Before you decide that Routines are broken, check these five simple reasons. In most cases, one of them is the answer.

  1. The routine's switch is off. Open the Routines list and look at the switch next to the routine's name. If it is off, tap it on. This is the most common reason of all.
  2. The start time is AM instead of PM, or the other way round. Open the routine, tap the trigger, and read the time slowly. A Sleep routine that starts at 10:00 AM will turn the phone quiet in the morning, not at night. Fix the time and save.
  3. The days are wrong. If the trigger only has Monday to Friday selected, the routine will not run on Saturday and Sunday. Check the days under the time, and select all seven days if your bedtime is the same every night.
  4. The Driving trigger never happens. The Driving routine only runs when the phone connects to the car's Bluetooth. If the phone is not connected to the car, or if the car was connected under a different name, the routine will not run. Check that the phone connects to the car as usual, and check that the routine names the same Bluetooth device. If the car has no Bluetooth, the Driving routine cannot work.
  5. Another mode is already on. If you turned on Do Not Disturb yourself, or if another mode is running, a routine may not change anything, because the phone is already quiet. This is not a fault. Turn the other mode off, and let the routine do the job.

If none of these five reasons fits, turn the routine off, wait a minute, turn it back on, and wait for the next trigger time. Routines are simple rules, and they need their trigger to really happen. A routine cannot run "just to test it". It runs when the time comes, or when the Bluetooth connects. If it still does not run after the next trigger, ask a family member to look at the routine with you, and read the trigger and the action aloud together. A second pair of eyes often spots a wrong time or a switched-off switch in seconds.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the routine's switch off. Saving a routine is not the same as turning it on. After saving, look at the Routines list and make sure the switch next to the routine's name is on. A routine with its switch off will never run.
  • Mixing up AM and PM. A Sleep routine that starts at 10:00 AM turns the phone quiet in the morning. Read the time slowly, and check AM and PM twice, before you save.
  • Choosing the wrong days. A routine that only runs on weekdays will not run on Saturday. If your bedtime is the same every night, select all seven days.
  • Expecting the Driving routine to work without Bluetooth. The routine needs the phone to connect to the car's Bluetooth. No Bluetooth, no trigger, no routine. That is how it is designed, not a fault.
  • Making the routine too complicated on the first day. One trigger and one action is enough. Do Not Disturb at bedtime is a good routine. Add brightness, or a second action, later, when the first routine is working.
  • Changing a routine while driving. Never look at the phone or change a routine while you are driving. Set routines up at home, before you travel.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a Mode and a Routine?

A Mode is a ready-made state, such as Sleep or Driving: the phone is quiet and calm. A Routine is a rule you make yourself: "when this happens, do that." A routine can turn a Mode on for you, at the right time, without you having to remember. You can use Modes on their own, by turning them on by hand, but a Routine is what makes the phone do it by itself.

Will my alarm still sound when the Sleep routine is running?

Yes, normally. An alarm is allowed to sound even when Do Not Disturb is on, because an alarm is not a message. Check it on the first morning, to be sure.

Can I have more than one routine?

Yes. Each routine has its own switch, and each one must be turned on by itself. Turning on one routine does not turn on the others.

What if I change my bedtime?

Open the routine, tap the trigger, change the time, and save. That is all. You do not need to delete the routine and make it again.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words may look a little different on your phone. That is normal. Samsung makes many Galaxy models. Some are A-series phones. Some are S-series phones. They do not all show the exact same words. Your phone company, called a carrier, can also change some words. Your country or region can change words too. And your One UI version matters. In October 2026, some phones use One UI 8, some use One UI 8.5, and some use One UI 9, depending on the model and the region. That is why this guide tells you to search for "Modes and Routines" in the Settings search box, instead of sending you down one fixed path. The search box works on every Galaxy phone, on every One UI version. Use it.

If you cannot find Modes and Routines in the search results, check two things. First, check that you spelled it as three words, "Modes and Routines", with spaces in between. Second, check that your phone is a Samsung Galaxy phone. Modes and Routines is a Samsung feature. It does not exist on other brands. If your phone is a Galaxy and the search still shows nothing, your phone may need a software update, or the feature may be named a little differently in your region. Ask a family member to look at the search with you, or take the phone to a Samsung service centre. Do not download an app that claims to be Modes and Routines. The real one is built into the phone, and it is reached through Settings.
