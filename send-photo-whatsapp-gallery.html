How to Send a Photo on WhatsApp from Your Galaxy Gallery

Tom is 70 years old. He grows tomatoes in his garden. This year, the tomatoes are large and red. They are the best he has grown in years. His brother David lives far away. David used to help Tom in the garden when they were young. Tom wants David to see the tomatoes. He wants David to smile. A message with words only would not show the colour. A photo will show it. Tom took a photo with his phone. Now he wants to send that photo to David on WhatsApp. He is not sure how. He does not want to send the wrong photo. He has many photos on his phone. Some are of his garden. Some are of bills and letters he photographed for his records. He wants to send only the tomato photo, to only David.

Your Galaxy phone stores your photos in an app called Gallery. Gallery is like a photo album. It shows your photos in order, newest first. WhatsApp is a separate app. It is not part of Samsung. It is not made by Samsung. WhatsApp is for messages and calls. You can send a photo from Gallery to WhatsApp. You do not need to copy the photo. You do not need to move it. You share it. Sharing means you pick a photo in Gallery, and you tell the phone to send a copy of it through WhatsApp. The original photo stays in Gallery. It is not deleted. It is not moved. Only a copy is sent.

This guide will help you send one photo, slowly and safely. You will learn how to find the right photo. You will learn how to check it before you send it. You will learn how to pick the right person. You will learn how to add a short message with the photo, so the person knows what they are looking at. Sending a photo with a short message is much kinder than sending a photo with no words. The person will know why you sent it. They will know what to look at.

Before you start, take a good photo, or pick a photo you already took. If you are taking a new photo, hold the phone steady. Make sure the light is good. Take one photo. Look at it. Is it clear? Is the thing you want to show in the middle? If it is blurry, take it again. It is better to take a second photo than to send a blurry one. Sit down before you send. Sending while you are walking can lead to mistakes. You may tap the wrong photo. You may tap the wrong person. A calm minute is worth it.

Send a photo from Gallery on WhatsApp, step by step

Follow these steps in order. The icons may look a little different on your phone, so this guide tells you what to look for.

  1. Find the Gallery app on your phone. Look for an icon that looks like a flower, or a picture of mountains and sun, or the word Gallery. Tap it once to open it. You should see your photos. The newest photos are usually at the top or at the end, depending on how your Gallery is set. Scroll slowly to find the photo you want.
  2. Tap the photo once to open it full screen. Look at it carefully. Is this the right photo? For Tom, is it the tomato photo, with red tomatoes and green leaves? Or is it a different photo, such as a bill or a blurry shot? Check the details. Zoom in a little by spreading two fingers apart on the screen if you need to see more detail. If it is the wrong photo, tap the back arrow and find the right one. Do not rush this check.
  3. Look for the Share button. It often looks like three small dots joined by lines, like a small triangle, or it may look like an arrow pointing up or to the side, or it may say Share. It is often at the bottom of the screen, or near the top. Tap it once. A list of apps will appear. This list is called the share sheet. It shows apps you can send the photo with.
  4. In the share list, look for WhatsApp. Look for the green icon with a white telephone inside a speech bubble. Tap WhatsApp once. WhatsApp will open. It will show a list of your chats and contacts. This is the moment to slow down. Do not tap the first name you see. Read the names carefully.
  5. Find the person you want to send the photo to. Scroll slowly. Look for their name and, if shown, their photo. For Tom, that is David. Tap David's name once. A tick or a highlight may appear next to the name. Check the name at the top of the next screen. Make sure it says David, and only David, unless you meant to send to a group. If you tapped the wrong person, tap the back arrow and pick again.
  6. Look for a button to go to the next step. It may be a small arrow, or the word Next, or a tick. Tap it once. Now you should see your photo again, inside WhatsApp, ready to send. Look at the photo once more. Is it still the right photo? This is your last easy check before sending.
  7. If you want, add a short message with the photo. Tap the box that says Add a caption, or Caption, or Message. Type a short sentence. For Tom, that could be: "Look at the tomatoes this year. They are the biggest yet." Keep it short. One or two sentences are enough. The caption helps David understand the photo at once. It also shows you sent it with care.
  8. Look for the Send button. It often looks like a paper plane, or a small arrow inside a green circle. It is often at the bottom right of the screen. Before you tap it, read the name at the top one last time. Read your caption. Look at the photo. If all three are right, tap Send once. Wait a second. The photo should appear in the chat with David. You will see a small tick, or two ticks, under the photo. That means it has been sent.

After you send, the photo stays in your Gallery. It is not deleted. You can send the same photo to another person later, if you want. You can also open the chat with David and see the photo there, with your caption under it. If David replies, his reply will appear under the photo. That is a normal chat. You can reply with words, or you can send another photo. One photo at a time is best when you are learning. Do not send ten photos at once. Send one. Wait for a reply. Then send the next one if you want to.

If the share list does not show WhatsApp, scroll the list sideways or down. Sometimes WhatsApp is hidden under a word like More. Tap More and look again. If WhatsApp is still not there, check that WhatsApp is on your phone. Ask a trusted family member to help you check. Do not download WhatsApp from a link in a message. Only get apps with help from someone you trust, from the normal app store on your phone.

Try it slowly: Tom sends the tomato photo to David

Let us practise with Tom. Tom does not send the photo to five people at once. He sends it to David only. He opens Gallery. He finds the tomato photo. The tomatoes are red. The leaves are green. The photo is clear. He taps Share. He taps WhatsApp. He finds David. He checks the name twice. He taps Next. He types: "Look at the tomatoes this year. They are the biggest yet." He checks the name, the photo, and the caption. He taps Send. The photo appears in the chat. Tom puts the phone down. He makes a cup of tea. A few minutes later, David replies: "They look wonderful. Well done." Tom smiles. He did it right. One photo. One person. One short message.

Now try a practice send yourself. Pick one photo you are proud of, or one photo that is simple and clear. Pick one person you trust. Tell them first, if you can: "I am going to practise sending you one photo on WhatsApp. Please tell me if it arrives." Then follow the steps above. Send only that one photo. Add a short caption. After you send, ask the person: "Did you get the photo? Is it the right one?" If they say yes, you have learned the skill. If they say no, or if the wrong photo arrived, do not worry. Look at what happened. Did you tap the wrong photo in Gallery? Did you tap the wrong name in WhatsApp? Knowing the mistake helps you avoid it next time.

Practise with a photo that does not matter much. Do not practise with a private photo, such as a photo of a bank letter, a passport, or a medical document. Those photos should almost never be sent on WhatsApp. Practise with a garden photo, a photo of a pet, or a photo of a meal. A safe practice photo means a mistake is not a problem. It is just a lesson. After two or three safe practice sends, you will feel confident. Then you can send photos that matter more, with the same careful checks.

Think about the caption. A caption is a gift to the person who gets the photo. It tells them what to look at. It tells them why you sent it. Without a caption, David might look at the tomato photo and wonder: is Tom asking for help? Is something wrong with the plants? With a caption, David knows at once: Tom is proud. He is sharing good news. Write captions as if you were speaking to the person. Short, warm, and clear. One sentence is often enough.

Common mistakes

  • Sending the wrong photo because you did not open it full screen first. Small thumbnails in Gallery can look alike. A photo of a red letter can look like a photo of red tomatoes at a glance. Always tap the photo to open it large. Look at it. Then tap Share.
  • Tapping the first name in the WhatsApp list without reading it. The first name is often the most recent chat, not the person you want. Scroll slowly. Read each name. Check the photo if one is shown. Make sure the name at the top is right before you tap Send.
  • Sending a photo to a group by mistake. A group chat has several people in it. A photo sent to a group is seen by everyone in the group. If you meant to send to one person, check that the chat shows only that one person's name. If you see several names, tap back and pick the one-to-one chat.
  • Sending many photos at once, before you have checked the first one. Ten photos arrive as a flood. The person does not know which one to look at first. Send one photo. Wait. If you want to send more, send them one or two at a time, with a short caption for each set.
  • Sending a blurry or dark photo without looking at it first. The person cannot see what you wanted to show. They may ask you to send it again. Check the photo in good light before you share it. If it is blurry, take a new photo. A clear photo is worth the extra minute.
  • Sending a private photo, such as a bank letter or an identity document, by mistake. Photos of private papers should not be sent on WhatsApp unless a trusted person or organisation has clearly asked for them, and you are sure who you are sending to. If you are not sure, do not send it. Ask a family member first. A wrong send cannot always be undone.

Frequently asked questions

Will sending a photo delete it from my Gallery?

No. Sharing a photo sends a copy. The original stays in Gallery. You can check after you send: open Gallery, find the photo, and it will still be there. If you want to delete the original later, you must delete it yourself in Gallery. Sending does not delete it.

Does sending a photo on WhatsApp cost money?

The send itself in WhatsApp is free to start, but it uses the internet. At home on Wi-Fi, that is usually included in your home internet. Out of the house, it uses mobile data. A single photo uses only a small amount of data. Many photos or videos use more. Use Wi-Fi for large sends when you can. If you are not sure about your data plan, ask a family member or your phone company.

Can I send a video the same way?

Yes, in a similar way. Find the video in Gallery. Tap Share. Tap WhatsApp. Pick the person. Add a caption if you want. Tap Send. Videos are larger than photos. They take longer to send. Keep practice videos short, under a minute, at first. A short video is easier to send and easier for the other person to watch.

What if I send the wrong photo to the wrong person?

Do not panic. Open the chat. You may see an option to delete the message for everyone, if you act quickly. Look for the message, press and hold it, and look for Delete, then Delete for everyone. This does not always work, and the person may have already seen it. If it was a private photo, tell a trusted family member at once and ask for help. If it was a harmless photo, send a short apology message: "Sorry, that was the wrong photo." Most people understand a mistake.

Can I send a photo to myself, to keep it safe?

Many people send a message to their own number in WhatsApp, or save a photo in a chat with themselves, as a reminder. That can work, but it is not a safe backup for important photos. For important photos, ask a family member to help you back them up in a proper way. Do not rely only on a chat with yourself. And never send private documents to yourself on WhatsApp as your only copy.

Why does my photo look smaller or less clear after I send it?

WhatsApp makes photos smaller so they send faster and use less data. That is normal. The photo in the chat may look a little less sharp than the original in Gallery. The original in Gallery stays sharp. If the person needs the full, sharp photo, for example to print it, ask a family member to help you send it in a different way. For looking on a phone screen, the WhatsApp version is usually fine.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words and icons may look a little different on your phone. That is normal. Samsung makes many Galaxy models. Some are A-series phones. Some are S-series phones. They do not all show the exact same words. Your phone company, called a carrier, can also change some words. Your country or region can change words too. And your One UI version matters. In October 2026, some phones use One UI 8, some use One UI 8.5, and some use One UI 9, depending on the model and the region. No one version is "the" version for every phone.

Gallery and WhatsApp can also look a little different from one phone to another, and after an update. The Share button may look like three dots joined by lines, or like an arrow, or it may say Share. The Send button in WhatsApp may look like a paper plane or a small arrow. Look for the shape and the meaning, not only the exact word. Look for a share shape in Gallery. Look for a send shape in WhatsApp. If you are in Settings and a word looks different, use the search box at the top of Settings and type the name of the setting. Take your time. Check the photo, check the name, and check the caption before you tap Send.
