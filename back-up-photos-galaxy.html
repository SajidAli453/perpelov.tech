How to Back Up Your Photos on Your Samsung Galaxy and Get Them Back on a New Phone

Salma is 67 years old. She has three grandchildren, and her phone is full of their photos. There is a photo of her youngest grandson taking his first steps in her garden. There is a photo of her granddaughter holding a birthday cake with seven candles. There is a photo of the whole family at Eid, all dressed up, all smiling. Salma looks at these photos every day.

Last month, Salma's phone fell off the kitchen table and onto the floor. The screen cracked. The phone still worked, but Salma was frightened. What if the phone had broken completely? All those photos would be gone. Her daughter said, "Mum, don't worry. Your photos are backed up." Salma did not know what that meant. Her daughter smiled and said, "Your phone does it by itself. Your photos are saved in another place, far away from the phone. Even if this phone breaks, the photos are safe."

That is what a backup is. It is a copy of your photos, kept somewhere safe, away from your phone. If your phone breaks, or if it is lost, or if you buy a new phone, you can get the photos back, because the copy is still there. This guide teaches you how to turn on the backup on your Samsung Galaxy phone, how to check that it really worked, and how to get your photos back on a new phone. It is not difficult. You do not need to understand computers. You only need your phone, your Wi-Fi, and ten quiet minutes. A backup needs the internet, so use Wi-Fi, not mobile data. If you are not sure your phone is on Wi-Fi, read How to Connect Your Galaxy to Wi-Fi first.

What a backup is, in plain words

Think of your photos as paper photographs in a box. If the box is lost, the photographs are lost. A backup is like making a copy of every photograph and keeping the copies in a second box, in a different house. If the first box is lost, the second box still has all the photographs. That is all a backup is. A copy, in a second place.

On your Galaxy phone, the second place is a storage service on the internet. The Photos app that comes with your Google account is the simplest way, and it is the way most people use to get their photos back on a new phone. There is also a Samsung backup, reached through your Samsung account, which can save your settings and messages as well. You do not need to understand the difference to use them. Both need you to be signed in to an account on your phone, and both need Wi-Fi.

Your account is the key. A backup is tied to the account you sign in with, not to the phone itself. If you sign in to a new phone with the same Google account, your photos come back. If you sign in with a different account, they will not come back, because the new account has no photos in it. So the most important thing in this whole guide is this: remember which Google account you use on your phone, and use the same one on your new phone.

Before you start: two things to check

Check two things before you turn on the backup. Both are quick, and both save trouble later.

  1. Your phone is on Wi-Fi. A backup sends many photos over the internet. On Wi-Fi, this is free and fast. On mobile data, it can be slow and it can cost money. Swipe down from the top of the screen and look for the Wi-Fi picture. If it is coloured, Wi-Fi is on. If you are not sure which Wi-Fi network you are on, open Settings, and use the search box at the top of Settings to search for "Wi-Fi". The name of your home Wi-Fi should be there. If the phone is not on Wi-Fi, connect it first. Read How to Connect Your Galaxy to Wi-Fi if you need the steps.
  2. Your phone has enough battery, or is charging. A first backup can take a while, because it has to send every photo you have ever taken. If the phone's battery runs out in the middle, the backup will stop and start again later. Plug the phone into its charger before you start.

If both of those are true, you are ready. You do not need to delete any photos first. You do not need to move anything. The backup copies your photos. It does not remove them from the phone. After the backup, your photos will still be on your phone, exactly as they were. The only difference is that a copy now exists somewhere safe as well.

Turn on photo backup, step by step

Follow these steps in order. Take your time. Nothing in these steps can delete your photos.

  1. Open the Photos app on your phone. It is the app with a small coloured flower or pinwheel picture. It may be called "Photos", or "Google Photos". If you cannot find it, swipe up from the bottom of the screen to see all your apps, and look for "Photos". Tap it once to open it.
  2. Look for your account picture or your first letter in a small circle. It is usually at the top right of the screen. It may be a small round photo of you, or a circle with the first letter of your name. Tap it once. A small menu should open, with your name and your email address at the top.
  3. Look for the words "Back up" or "Backup" in the menu. If it says "Backup is on", your photos are already being backed up, and you can skip to the next part of this guide, which shows you how to check it. If it says "Backup is off", or "Turn on backup", tap that line.
  4. The phone should show a short explanation. It may say that your photos will be saved to your Google account, and that you can see them on any device where you sign in with the same account. Read it, slowly. If it asks which quality to use, choose the one the phone suggests.
  5. Tap "Turn on" or "Confirm". The phone should now start backing up. You may see a small message saying "Backup in progress", or a small spinning circle. That is normal. You can leave the app now. The backup keeps running in the background, even if you use other apps, or if the phone's screen turns off.
  6. Leave the phone on Wi-Fi and charging for a while. The first backup is the longest one, because it has to send every photo you have. If you have hundreds of photos, it can take an hour or more. You do not need to wait. Go and do something else. The phone will work through the list, one photo at a time, as long as it stays on Wi-Fi and has battery.
  7. After the backup, the phone only sends new photos. When you take a new photo, the phone sends a copy of that one photo, which takes a few seconds. After the first backup, you never need to think about it again.

That is the whole job for the Photos app. If your phone also asks you about a Samsung backup, in Settings, that is a second safety net. It can save your phone's settings, your messages, and your apps, as well as your photos. You can turn it on too, the same way: open Settings, use the search box at the top to search for "Back up", and follow the instructions on the screen. It will ask you to sign in with your Samsung account. If you do not have a Samsung account, you can skip it. The Photos backup alone is enough to keep your photos safe.

Check that the backup really worked

Turning on the backup is only half the job. You should also check that it really worked. A check takes one minute, and it gives you peace of mind.

  1. Open the Photos app again. Tap your small account picture or letter at the top right, the same as before. Look at the backup line. It should say something like "Backup complete", or "Backed up", with a date or a time. That means every photo on your phone has a copy saved. If it says "Backup in progress", with a number of photos still to go, the backup is still running. Wait a little longer, and check again later.
  2. Pick one photo and check it. Open one photo that matters to you. Look at the top of the screen, or tap the photo once, and look for a small cloud picture, or the words "Backed up". A small cloud with a tick means that this photo has a copy saved.
  3. Check from another device, if you can. If you have a computer, or a family member's phone, open the Photos website or app there, and sign in with the same Google account. Your photos should be there. If they are, your backup is real, and your photos are safe even if your phone breaks tomorrow.

If the backup line says "Waiting for Wi-Fi", or "Backup paused", the phone is telling you that it is not on Wi-Fi right now. Connect to Wi-Fi, plug the phone into its charger, and check again in an hour. If the phone says your storage is full, read How to Free Up Space on Your Galaxy Without Deleting Your Photos.

Get your photos back on a new phone

One day, you will have a new phone. It may be because your old phone broke, or because you chose a new one. Either way, getting your photos back is simple, as long as you use the same Google account.

  1. When you first turn on the new phone, it will ask you to sign in. Sign in with the same Google account you used on your old phone. This is the most important step. Check the email address on the screen, letter by letter, and make sure it is the same one as on your old phone.
  2. Wait for the new phone to finish its first setup. It may take a few minutes. The phone may ask you about a backup, and whether you want to restore your old phone's data. Say yes. The phone will then bring back your apps, your settings, and your messages, as well as your photos.
  3. Open the Photos app on the new phone. Your photos should start to appear, newest first. They may not all appear at once. Leave the new phone on Wi-Fi and charging, and check again in an hour.
  4. If some photos seem to be missing, do not panic. First, check that you are signed in with the right account. Open the Photos app, tap your small account picture at the top right, and read the email address. Is it the same as on your old phone? Second, check that the new phone is on Wi-Fi. Third, wait a little longer.

There is also a tool called Smart Switch, made by Samsung, which can move everything from your old phone to your new phone, including your photos. It works with a cable between the two phones, or over Wi-Fi, with both phones next to each other. When you set up the new phone, it should offer Smart Switch as one of the choices. If your old phone is broken and will not turn on, Smart Switch cannot help, and the Photos backup is the way to get your photos back.

Practise with Salma: one photo, backed up and found again

Let us practise with Salma and one photo. Salma has a photo of her youngest grandson taking his first steps in her garden. It is her favourite photo. She opens the Photos app, and she taps the photo, so it fills the screen. Then she taps her small account picture at the top right, and she reads the backup line. It says "Backup complete". Good. She taps the photo again, and she sees a small cloud with a tick. That photo has a copy saved. Now Salma imagines she has a new phone. She signs in with the same Google account. She opens the Photos app. She waits. After a few minutes, the garden photo appears, big and clear. Nothing was lost.

Now try the same practice yourself, with one photo that does not matter much. Open the Photos app. Turn on the backup, if it is not on already. Wait for the backup line to say "Backup complete". Then open one photo, and look for the small cloud with a tick. That is your proof. Your photos are safe.

If a photo does not appear, check these things first

Before you decide that your photos are lost, check these six simple reasons. In most cases, one of them is the answer.

  1. You are signed in with a different account. This is the most common reason of all. A backup belongs to the account you signed in with. If the new phone uses a different Google account, it will show no photos, because that account has none. Check the email address in the Photos app, on both phones, and make sure they match, exactly.
  2. The backup is still running. If the backup line says "Backup in progress", with photos still to go, the photos are not lost. They are still on their way. Leave the phone on Wi-Fi and charging, and check again in an hour.
  3. The phone is not on Wi-Fi. A backup needs Wi-Fi. If the phone is on mobile data, or if Wi-Fi is off, the backup will wait. Connect to Wi-Fi, and the backup should start again by itself.
  4. Your storage is full. The safe place on the internet has a limit. If it is full, new photos cannot be backed up. The phone will usually tell you. Read How to Free Up Space on Your Galaxy Without Deleting Your Photos, and check how much storage you have left in How to Check How Much Battery and Storage You Have Left on Your Galaxy.
  5. The photo is in a folder that is not backed up. The Photos app backs up the photos you take with the camera. Photos in some other folders, such as pictures saved from messages, may not be backed up unless you turn that on too. In the Photos app, look for the backup settings, and check which folders are included.
  6. The new phone needs more time. A new phone has to fetch every photo, one by one. If you have thousands of photos, it can take hours. The photos appear as they arrive. Wait, and check again later, before you decide that anything is missing.

If none of these six reasons fits, ask a family member to look at the backup line in the Photos app with you, and to check the account and the Wi-Fi with you. A second pair of eyes often spots a different email address in seconds. Do not download an app that claims to recover lost photos for money. The real backup is built into the phone, and it is reached through the Photos app. An app that promises to find lost photos is not needed, and it may be a scam.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking the photos are safe because they are on the phone. Photos on the phone are not backed up. They are only on the phone. If the phone breaks, or is lost, they are gone. A backup is the copy somewhere else. Turn it on, and check that it says "Backup complete".
  • Signing in with a different account on the new phone. The account is the key. If the new phone uses a different Google account, your photos will not come back. Check the email address, letter by letter, when you sign in to the new phone.
  • Turning off Wi-Fi and expecting the backup to finish. A backup needs Wi-Fi. On mobile data, it will wait, or it will use your mobile data, which can cost money. Keep the phone on Wi-Fi while the first backup runs.
  • Deleting photos from the phone to save space, and thinking the backup keeps them. Be careful here. If you delete a photo from the Photos app, the backup may delete its copy too, after a while. The backup is a copy, not a separate hiding place. If you need space, read How to Free Up Space on Your Galaxy Without Deleting Your Photos first.
  • Forgetting the account password. Your photos are tied to your Google account. If you forget the password, you cannot sign in to get them back. Write the password down on paper, and keep the paper somewhere safe at home. Do not share it with strangers.
  • Believing an app that promises to recover lost photos. If your photos were never backed up, no app can bring them back from a broken phone. The time to back up is before the phone breaks. An app that claims to recover photos for money is not needed, and it may be a scam.
  • Never checking the backup line. Turning on the backup is not enough. Check, once, that it says "Backup complete". That one check is the difference between thinking your photos are safe and knowing they are safe.

Frequently asked questions

Will a backup delete the photos from my phone?

No. A backup copies your photos. It does not remove them. The only difference is that a copy now exists somewhere safe as well. If you want to free space on your phone, read the guide about freeing space without deleting photos, and follow its steps carefully.

Do I have to pay for a backup?

The Photos app gives you some free storage with your Google account. For most people, with a few hundred photos, the free storage is enough. If you take very many photos, or very many videos, the free storage can fill up, and the phone will tell you. You can then choose to pay for more storage, or to make room. You do not have to pay anything to turn on the backup and to use the free storage.

Can other people see my backed-up photos?

No, not unless you share them. Your backed-up photos are tied to your Google account, and only someone signed in to that account can see them. Do not share your account password with anyone. If a message asks for your password, do not give it.

What happens to my photos if I lose my phone?

If your photos are backed up, they are safe. Buy or borrow a new phone, sign in with the same Google account, open the Photos app, and your photos will come back, over Wi-Fi. If your photos were never backed up, and the phone is lost, they are gone. That is why this guide asks you to turn on the backup now, before anything goes wrong, and to check that it says "Backup complete".

Does the backup also save my videos?

Yes, in most cases. The Photos app backs up videos as well as photos, using the same backup. Videos are bigger than photos, so they take longer to back up, and they use more storage. If your storage fills up, videos are often the reason.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words may look a little different on your phone. That is normal. Samsung makes many Galaxy models. Some are A-series phones. Some are S-series phones. They do not all show the exact same words. Your phone company, called a carrier, can also change some words. Your country or region can change words too. And your One UI version matters. In October 2026, some phones use One UI 8, some use One UI 8.5, and some use One UI 9, depending on the model and the region. No one version is "the" version for every phone. That is why this guide tells you to look for the words "Back up" in the Photos app, and to use the search box at the top of Settings and type "Back up" if you cannot find a setting.

If you cannot find the backup line in the Photos app at all, check two things. First, check that you opened the Photos app, not the Gallery app. The Gallery app shows your photos, but the backup lives in the Photos app, with the small coloured flower or pinwheel picture. Second, check that you are signed in to a Google account on the phone. Open Settings, use the search box at the top to search for "Accounts", and look at the list of accounts.
