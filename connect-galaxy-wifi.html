How to Connect Your Galaxy to Wi-Fi

Mary is 69. She visited her daughter for the weekend. Her daughter said, just join our Wi-Fi, and your phone will work faster. Mary smiled and nodded, but inside she felt unsure. She did not want to press the wrong thing. She did not want to break her daughter's internet. She also did not know the password, and she did not want to keep asking.

Wi-Fi is simply a wireless connection to the internet in one place, such as a home, a library, or a café. When your phone joins Wi-Fi, it uses that place's internet instead of your mobile data. That can make web pages load faster, and it can save mobile data. You do not need to understand how it works. You only need to know how to pick the right network name and type the password with care.

This guide shows you how to join Wi-Fi in Settings, and how to do it from the Quick settings panel. It also shows you what to do when the password does not work, when you cannot see the network name, or when the phone says it is connected but web pages will not load. Take your time. Passwords are fussy about capital letters and small details, so slow typing helps more than fast typing.

One kind warning before you start. Only join Wi-Fi networks you know and trust, such as your own home, a family member's home, or a library you visit often. In a public place, ask a member of staff for the correct network name. Do not join a network just because its name looks free or friendly. If you are unsure, ask the person who owns the Wi-Fi. They will be glad you asked.

Step by step: connect in Settings

  1. Open Settings. Find the Settings app on your home screen or in your apps list. It often looks like a gear or a cog wheel. Tap it. The Settings screen will open, with a list of words.
  2. Tap Connections. Look down the list for the word Connections. Tap it. This is where your phone keeps Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and mobile data settings together.
  3. Tap Wi-Fi. On the Connections screen, tap Wi-Fi. This is the exact path: Settings, then Connections, then Wi-Fi. You will see a switch at the top of the Wi-Fi screen. If the switch is off, tap it to turn Wi-Fi on. The phone will start looking for networks nearby.
  4. Wait for the list of networks. After a few seconds, a list of network names will appear. Each name belongs to a Wi-Fi router nearby. The list may show names from neighbours too. Look for the name you want. At home, it is often the name on the label of your router box. At a family member's house, ask them to point to the right name on your screen.
  5. Tap the network name you want. Tap the name once. A box will appear and ask for a password. This password is sometimes called the Wi-Fi key or the network password. It is not your phone PIN, and it is not your email password. It belongs to that Wi-Fi network only.
  6. Type the password slowly and with care. Passwords care about capital letters and small letters. A capital A is not the same as a small a. Numbers matter too. Type one character at a time. If the password is written on a card or on the router label, read it aloud softly as you type, one group at a time. If your phone shows a small eye picture in the password box, tap it to see what you typed. That helps you spot a mistake before you go on.
  7. Tap Connect. After you type the password, tap the Connect button. Wait a few seconds. Do not tap Connect again and again. The phone will try to join the network. If the password is right, the phone will join and the network name will show words like Connected. A small Wi-Fi fan picture will appear at the top of your screen.
  8. Try a web page. Open your web browser. Try to open a simple web page you know. If the page loads, you are joined. Put the phone down and enjoy it. If the page does not load, go to the help section below before you type the password again.

Step by step: connect from the Quick settings panel

  1. Swipe down from the top with one finger. A small panel will appear. Swipe down again to see more icons. Look for the Wi-Fi picture, which looks like a small fan made of curved lines.
  2. Press and hold the Wi-Fi picture. Do not just tap it once. Press and hold it for a moment. The Wi-Fi settings screen will open. From here, the steps are the same as above: pick the network name, type the password slowly, and tap Connect.
  3. Check the Wi-Fi picture at the top of your screen. When you are joined, the fan picture will be filled in and may show small lines. If the picture is empty or has a small cross, you are not joined yet. Go back to Settings, then Connections, then Wi-Fi, and check again.

Try it slowly: Mary at her daughter's house

Mary's daughter wrote the Wi-Fi password on a piece of paper in large letters. Mary read it out loud, one part at a time. She typed the first part, then she stopped and checked it. She typed the next part, then she checked again. The first time, the phone said the password was wrong. Mary did not panic. She looked at the paper again. She had typed a small o where the password had a zero. The two look very alike.

She tried again, slowly, with the eye picture turned on so she could see what she typed. This time the phone joined at once. The Wi-Fi fan appeared at the top of her screen. Her daughter showed her how to check: Mary opened her browser, and a web page loaded quickly. Mary smiled. She had done it herself, with a little help and a lot of patience.

You can ask for the same help. Ask the owner of the Wi-Fi to write the password in large, clear letters. Ask them to stay near you while you type it the first time. There is no shame in asking. Passwords are made to be hard to guess, so they are also hard to type. Slow typing, and checking each part, will help you more than any trick.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up a capital letter and a small letter. Wi-Fi passwords care about capitals. If the paper shows a capital letter, type a capital. If it shows a small letter, type a small one. Check before you tap Connect.
  • Mixing up the letter O and the number zero. These two look almost the same in many fonts. The same is true for the letter l and the number one. If the password fails, check those characters first.
  • Joining the wrong network name. Neighbours' networks often have similar names. If you join the wrong one, your password will not work, or the web will not load. Ask the owner to point to the exact name on your screen.
  • Typing the phone PIN instead of the Wi-Fi password. These are different codes. The Wi-Fi password belongs to the router, not to your phone. The router label or the owner of the home will have it.
  • Tapping Connect many times in a hurry. Tapping again and again does not help the phone join faster. It can make the phone try with a half-typed password. Type it fully, check it, tap Connect once, and wait.
  • Forgetting to turn Wi-Fi on at all. If Wi-Fi is off, no network list will appear. Check the switch at the top of the Wi-Fi screen in Settings, then Connections, then Wi-Fi. The switch must be on before you can pick a network.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to type the password every time I come home?

No, not usually. Once your phone has joined your home Wi-Fi, it will remember it. Next time you come home, it should join by itself, as long as Wi-Fi is on. If it does not join, open Settings, then Connections, then Wi-Fi, and tap your home network name.

What if I cannot see the network name in the list?

Move closer to the router box. Walls and distance can hide a network. Wait a few seconds and look again. Swipe down on the Wi-Fi list to refresh it, if your phone shows that choice. If the name still does not appear, ask the owner if the router is on and if the name is hidden. Some routers hide their name, and the owner will need to help you add it.

It says Connected, but web pages will not load. What do I do?

First, wait a minute. Some networks need a moment. Then try turning Wi-Fi off and on again in Settings, then Connections, then Wi-Fi. Open the browser again. If it still does not load, ask the owner if their internet is working on another device. The problem may be with the internet in the house, not with your phone.

Is Wi-Fi safe in a café or a library?

Use care. A café or library network is shared with strangers. Do not do banking on a public network if you can avoid it. If you must check a bank account, use your mobile data instead, by turning Wi-Fi off for a short time. For reading news, looking at photos, or watching a video, a known café network is usually fine. When in doubt, ask a member of staff which network is the real one.

How do I forget a network I joined by mistake?

Open Settings, then Connections, then Wi-Fi. Tap the network name you want to forget. Look for words like Forget. Tap Forget. Your phone will no longer join that network by itself. You can join it again later if you type the password again.

If the words on your screen look different

Most Galaxy phones use the same main path for Wi-Fi: Settings, then Connections, then Wi-Fi. That path is the same on many models. Still, small words around it can differ. One phone may say Wi-Fi, another may show the same word with a slightly different layout. The list of networks may look a little different. The Connect button may sit in a different place in the box. The password box may show the eye picture on the right, or it may hide it until you tap the box.

Your carrier or your region can also change small details. A network name may show extra words, such as the name of a guest network. A guest network often has the word Guest in its name, and it may need an extra step, such as tapping Agree on a web page that opens after you join. If a step looks different, do not guess. Open Settings, use the search box at the top, and type Wi-Fi. Tap the result that says Wi-Fi. That search helps when the order of items differs because of your model, your region, your carrier, or your One UI version, which may be One UI 8, One UI 8.5, or One UI 9. Ask the owner of the Wi-Fi if you are still unsure about the name or the password.
