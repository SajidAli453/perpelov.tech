How to Use the Edge Panel on Your Samsung Galaxy (Favourite Apps and Tools One Swipe Away)

Khalid is 70 years old. He uses his Samsung Galaxy phone every day, but only for a few things: he calls his children, he reads messages on WhatsApp, he looks at photos of his grandchildren, and he uses the torch when the power goes out at night. Every time he wants one of these things, he has to look for it. He swipes through his home screen and the list of all his apps, looking for the green WhatsApp picture, or the small torch icon. Sometimes he taps the wrong thing, and an app opens that he did not want. It is not hard, but it is slow, and it is annoying, especially when he wants the torch in the dark.

Last week, his grandson showed him something new. At the right edge of the screen, there is a thin, pale handle, like a small tab. His grandson dragged it towards the middle of the screen, with one finger. A panel slid out from the edge, like a small drawer. Inside it were the apps Khalid uses most: WhatsApp, the Phone app, the Camera, and the Gallery. Underneath them were tools: a torch, a ruler, a compass, and a timer. Khalid tapped WhatsApp, and it opened at once.

That small drawer is called the Edge Panel. It is a shortcut shelf at the edge of your screen. You put your favourite apps and tools in it, and then you can reach them from anywhere on your phone, with one swipe, without going back to the home screen. This guide teaches you how to turn it on, open it, fill it, move its handle, and turn it off again.

What the Edge Panel is

Think of your phone screen as a desk. Your home screen is the top of the desk, and the list of all your apps is a drawer under it. The Edge Panel is a second, smaller drawer, fixed to the side of the desk. You open it with one pull, take what you need, and push it closed again.

The panel opens from a thin handle at the edge of the screen, usually on the right edge, about halfway down. It is a small, pale, rounded tab. When you drag it towards the middle of the screen, the panel slides out. When you tap something in the panel, the panel closes by itself, and the app or tool you tapped opens.

The panel holds two kinds of things: apps you use most, such as WhatsApp, the Phone app, the Camera, and the Gallery, and tools, such as a torch, a ruler, a compass, and a timer. The exact tools on your phone may be a little different. Samsung changes the list from time to time, and some phones show more tools than others.

One important thing: the Edge Panel installs nothing. It does not download apps or buy anything. It only shows shortcuts to apps and tools already on your phone. Adding WhatsApp to the panel does not install WhatsApp twice, and removing it does not delete WhatsApp.

Turn the Edge Panel on first

On many Galaxy phones, the Edge Panel is already on, and the handle is already visible at the edge of the screen. Look at the right edge of your screen now, about halfway down. Can you see a thin, pale tab? If you can, the panel is already on, and you can skip to the next part of this guide. If you cannot see a tab, the panel may be off, and you need to turn it on first.

  1. Open Settings. It is the app with a small gear picture. If you cannot find it, swipe down from the top of the screen and look for the small gear at the top right, or search for "Settings" in your list of all apps.
  2. At the top of Settings, there is a search box. Tap it, and type Edge panels. Tap the result with those words. This is the safest way to find the setting, because its place in the Settings list can be different on different phones.
  3. You should now see a screen about Edge panels, with a switch at the top. It looks like a small oval with a circle inside it. If the circle is on the right and the switch is coloured, the panel is on. If the circle is on the left and the switch is grey, it is off. Tap the switch once to turn it on.
  4. Go back to your home screen. Look at the right edge of the screen, about halfway down. You should now see the thin, pale handle. It is easy to miss in bright light, so tilt the phone slightly, and it should be easier to see.

If the search for "Edge panels" shows no result, try "Edge panel" without the "s", or search for "Display" and look for a line about Edge panels inside it. The exact words can be different on different One UI versions. The important part is the switch. Once it is on, the handle appears.

Open the panel: one swipe from the handle

Opening the panel is a small gesture. Do it slowly, the first few times, and it will soon feel natural.

  1. Find the handle at the edge of your screen. It is the thin, pale tab, usually on the right edge, about halfway down. Put the tip of one finger on it. Do not tap it. Tapping does nothing. You need to drag it.
  2. Drag it slowly towards the middle of the screen, about two or three centimetres. Keep your finger on the screen while you drag. The panel should slide out from the edge, like a drawer opening. When you see it, lift your finger.
  3. Look at what is inside. You should see a column of apps, with their pictures, and perhaps a row of tools underneath. The first time you open the panel, it may show apps you have never chosen. That is normal. The phone puts a few common apps there to start with.
  4. To close the panel without opening anything, tap anywhere outside the panel, on the dimmed part of the screen. Or tap an app in the panel, and the app opens, and the panel closes by itself.

If the panel does not open on your first try, you probably tapped the handle instead of dragging it. Try again, a little slower. Put your finger exactly on the handle, press gently, and drag in a straight line towards the middle. Most people need three or four tries before the gesture feels easy.

Add your favourite apps to the panel

The panel is only useful if it holds the apps you actually use. A panel full of apps you never open is no better than the list of all your apps. So take five minutes, and fill it with your own favourites. Khalid put four apps in his: WhatsApp, the Phone app, the Camera, and the Gallery. Pick the apps you reach for most often, and leave the rest out.

  1. Open the panel, by dragging the handle towards the middle of the screen.
  2. Look at the bottom of the panel. You should see a small pencil picture, or the word "Edit", or three small dots. Tap it. The panel should change into editing mode, and you may see a list of all your apps appear.
  3. Look for the apps you want. You should see a list of your apps, with a small circle or a plus sign next to each one. Tap the apps you want in the panel. Each app you tap should appear in the panel, or get a small tick next to it.
  4. When you have chosen your apps, look for "Done", or an arrow back, or a tick at the top of the screen. Tap it. The panel should go back to its normal look, with your chosen apps inside it.
  5. Open the panel again, and check. Are your apps there? Tap one, and does it open? If yes, you have done it. If an app is missing, open the editing mode again, and add it.

Keep the list short: four or five apps is quick to use, twenty is just another long list. Start with three — the app you call people with, the app you message people with, and the Camera — and add more only when real use shows you need them. In editing mode you can also drag an app up or down to change its order.

Use the tools in the panel

Underneath the apps, the panel may show a row of small tools — tiny helpers built into the phone. The exact tools on your phone may be different from the tools on someone else's phone. Here are the tools you are most likely to see.

  • Torch. This turns on the phone's light. It is useful in the dark, when the power goes out, or when you are looking for something under a table. Tap it once to turn it on, and again to turn it off. For a full guide, read How to Use the Flashlight (Torch) on Your Samsung Galaxy.
  • Ruler. This turns your screen into a short ruler. Hold the edge of the phone against the thing you want to measure, and the screen shows the length. It is not as exact as a real ruler, but it is handy for a rough size.
  • Compass. This shows you which way is north, south, east, and west, using the phone's built-in compass. Hold the phone flat, and wait a second for the needle to settle.
  • Timer. This counts down a number of minutes you choose, and then makes a sound. It is useful in the kitchen, when you are cooking rice or boiling an egg. Set the minutes, tap start, and put the phone down.

To use a tool, open the panel and tap it once. To stop it, tap it again, or close the panel. If a tool you expected is not there, your phone may simply not show it. And check the torch is really off before you put the phone in your pocket — it uses battery and makes the phone warm.

Move the handle and change how it looks

The handle sits at the edge of the screen, and on most phones it is in a good place already. But your hand is your own. If the handle sits where your thumb rests when you hold the phone, you may open the panel by accident, again and again. You can move it, and you can also make it easier, or harder, to see.

  1. Open the panel, by dragging the handle towards the middle of the screen.
  2. Look at the bottom of the panel for a small pencil picture, or the word "Edit", or a small gear picture. Tap it. You should see settings for the panel, including choices about the handle.
  3. Look for a choice about the handle's position. It may say "Handle position", or "Handle settings". Tap it, and drag the handle up or down the edge of the screen to a place that suits your hand. A good place is where your thumb naturally rests when you hold the phone, a little below the middle.
  4. Look for a choice about how the handle looks. It may let you change its size, from small to large, or its transparency, from solid to see-through. A larger handle is easier to find and to grab. A more transparent handle is less noticeable. Try one setting, use it for a day, and change it if it does not feel right.
  5. When you are happy, go back. The panel should remember your choices.

Remove an app from the panel

Sooner or later, you will want to take an app out of the panel. Perhaps you stopped using it, or you added it by mistake. Removing an app from the panel is easy, and it is safe. It does not delete the app from your phone. The app stays on your home screen, and in the list of all your apps. Only the shortcut in the panel goes away.

  1. Open the panel, by dragging the handle towards the middle of the screen.
  2. Look for the pencil picture, or the word "Edit", at the bottom of the panel. Tap it. The panel should change into editing mode.
  3. Find the app you want to remove. Tap the small minus sign on it, or the small cross, or tap the app to untick it. The exact sign can be different on different phones, but it is usually a small minus or a small cross on the corner of the app's picture.
  4. Look for "Done", or a tick, or an arrow back, and tap it. Open the panel again, and check. The app should be gone from the panel, and still on your phone, in the list of all your apps.

Practise with Khalid: fill the panel in five minutes

Let us practise with Khalid. He turns on the panel in Settings, using the search box and the words "Edge panels". He drags the handle towards the middle, and the panel slides out. He taps the pencil at the bottom, and he adds four apps: WhatsApp, the Phone app, the Camera, and the Gallery. He taps "Done". He opens the panel again, and his four apps are there. He taps WhatsApp, and it opens at once. The whole thing took him five minutes.

Now try the same practice yourself. Turn the panel on. Open it. Add three apps: the app you call people with, the app you message people with, and the Camera. Close the panel. Open it again, and tap one of your three apps. Does it open? If yes, you have done it. Use the panel for a week, and add a fourth app only when real use shows you need it.

If the panel does not work

Sometimes the panel does not behave the way you expect. Do not panic. Work through these checks, in order. Most problems are one of these four.

  1. You cannot see the handle. First, check that the panel is on. Open Settings, search for "Edge panels", and look at the switch. If it is off, turn it on. Second, look again, slowly. The handle is thin and pale. Look at the right edge, about halfway down. Third, check the left edge too. Some phones put it there. If it is still not there, restart the phone, and look again.
  2. You can see the handle, but the panel does not open. You are probably tapping the handle instead of dragging it. Put your finger exactly on the handle, press gently, and drag in a straight line towards the middle of the screen, about two or three centimetres. Try it three or four times, slowly, before you decide it is broken.
  3. The panel opens, but your apps are not there. The panel may be showing its first, automatic list, not your list. Open the editing mode, with the pencil at the bottom, and add your apps, as this guide shows. If you added them already, check that you tapped "Done" after adding them. If you closed the editing mode without tapping "Done", the phone may not have saved your choices.
  4. The panel opens by itself, again and again. The handle is probably sitting where your thumb rests when you hold the phone. Move the handle to a different place on the edge, higher or lower, as this guide shows. Or make the handle smaller. If it still opens by itself, turn the panel off for a while, and turn it back on when you need it.

Turn the panel off again

If you try the panel for a week, and you decide you do not like it, you can turn it off. Nothing is lost. Your apps stay where they are, and your home screen does not change. Open Settings, search for "Edge panels", tap the result, and tap the switch once to turn it off. If you change your mind later, turn the switch back on. Your apps should still be in the panel, as you left them.

Common mistakes

  • Tapping the handle instead of dragging it. Tapping does nothing. You must put your finger on the handle and drag it towards the middle of the screen. Do it slowly, the first few times, until your hand learns the movement.
  • Filling the panel with twenty apps. A panel with twenty apps is just another long list. Keep it to four or five apps, the ones you use every day.
  • Thinking that removing an app from the panel deletes it. It does not. The app stays on your phone, on your home screen, and in the list of all your apps. Only the shortcut in the panel goes away. You can add it back any time.
  • Forgetting to tap "Done" after editing. If you add apps and then close the editing mode without tapping "Done", the phone may not save your choices. Always look for "Done", or a tick, and tap it before you leave the editing screen.
  • Leaving the torch on. The torch is the tool people forget most often. It uses battery, and it makes the phone warm. Always check that the light is really off before you put the phone in your pocket or your bag.
  • Putting the handle where your thumb rests. If the panel keeps opening by itself, the handle is in the wrong place for your hand. Move it higher or lower on the edge, or make it smaller.
  • Downloading an app to add an edge panel. You do not need one. The real panel is built into the phone. An app that promises to add one is not needed, and it may be a scam. Use Settings and the words "Edge panels".

Frequently asked questions

Does the Edge Panel use extra battery?

Only a very small amount. The panel itself is just a list of shortcuts, and it does nothing while it is closed. The apps and tools you open from it use battery in the normal way, just as they do when you open them from the home screen.

Can I put the panel on the left edge instead of the right?

On many Galaxy phones, yes. Open the panel settings, look for the handle position, and drag the handle to the left edge, or look for a choice between left and right. If your phone does not offer the choice, the handle stays on the right edge.

Will the panel work inside every app?

Almost every app, yes. You can open the panel from the home screen, from WhatsApp, from the Camera, and from most other apps. A few games that fill the whole screen may hide the handle while they are open. If the handle disappears inside a game, close the game, and the handle should come back.

What happens to the panel if I restart my phone?

Nothing. The panel stays on, and your apps stay in it, exactly as you left them. You do not need to set it up again after a restart. The switch stays on, the handle stays where you put it, and the list stays the same.

Can someone else's apps appear in my panel?

No. The panel shows only the apps on your phone, and only the apps you choose. It does not download apps by itself. If an app appears in the panel that you did not add, it was put there by the phone when the panel was first turned on. Open the editing mode, and remove it, as this guide shows.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words may look a little different on your phone. That is normal. Samsung makes many Galaxy models — A-series and S-series — and they do not all show the exact same words. Your phone company, called a carrier, can also change some words, and so can your country or region. And your One UI version matters. In October 2026, some phones use One UI 8, some use One UI 8.5, and some use One UI 9, depending on the model and the region. That is why this guide tells you to search for "Edge panels" in the search box at the top of Settings, and to look for the words "Edit", "Done", and the pencil picture.

If you cannot find "Edge panels" in the Settings search, check two things. First, check that you typed the words correctly: "Edge panels", with an "s" at the end, or "Edge panel" without it. Second, check that you are searching inside Settings, not inside the list of all your apps. If the search still shows nothing, try searching for "Display", and look for a line about Edge panels inside the Display screen.
