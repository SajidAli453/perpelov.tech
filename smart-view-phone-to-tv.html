How to Connect Your Samsung Galaxy Phone to Your TV with Smart View

Ahmed is 70 years old. His grandchildren visit every Sunday. Last Sunday, his granddaughter took a photo of the whole family in the garden. Ahmed wanted to show it to everyone in the room. So he held his phone up, and walked around the room, showing the small screen to one person at a time. His daughter said, "Dad, just put it on the TV." Ahmed laughed. The TV is a big screen on the wall. The phone is a small screen in his hand. How could the phone's picture appear on the TV? There was no cable. It sounded like magic, or like a job for a young person.

It is neither. Ahmed's phone can already do it. On a Samsung Galaxy phone, there is a feature called Smart View. It shows your phone's screen on your TV, without a cable. Whatever is on your phone, a photo, a video, a family message, appears on the big TV screen, so everyone in the room can see it at the same time. You do not need to buy anything. You do not need a cable. You do not need a young person to do it for you. You only need a TV that can do screen mirroring, and your phone and your TV on the same Wi-Fi network. This guide teaches you how to check those two things, how to connect, how to stop, and what to do when the TV does not appear in the list.

First, a plain explanation of what Smart View does. It copies your phone's screen onto the TV. It is a mirror. When you swipe on the phone, the TV shows the swipe. When you open a photo, the TV shows the photo. The TV is not playing the video by itself. The phone is playing it, and the TV is showing a copy. That is why the phone's screen stays on while you mirror. If the phone's screen turns off, the mirror turns off too. Smart View is not the same as casting a video from one app. Some video apps have their own small cast button, and they send only the video to the TV, while the phone stays free for other things. Smart View sends the whole screen. For showing photos to a room full of family, the whole screen is what you want.

You do not need to learn everything on this page in one sitting. Read the part about the two requirements first. Then follow the steps to connect, once, while you are sitting near the TV. If it works the first time, you will know how it feels, and the next time it will take less than a minute. If it does not work the first time, do not worry. The troubleshooting part at the end covers the common reasons, and most of them are simple: the Wi-Fi, the TV, or the distance.

Check the two requirements first

Smart View needs two things to be true. If either one is not true, the phone will not find the TV, and no amount of tapping will fix it. So check both before you start. It takes two minutes, and it saves a lot of frustration.

  1. Your TV must support screen mirroring. Most Samsung smart TVs made in the last few years support it. Many other smart TVs support it too, under names like "Screen Mirroring", "Miracast", or "Smart View". An old TV with no smart features, and no extra device plugged into it, usually cannot do it. If you are not sure, look at the TV's menu, or its instruction book, for the words "Screen Mirroring" or "Smart View". If your TV is a Samsung smart TV, it almost certainly supports it. If your TV is very old, or if it is not a smart TV at all, Smart View will not work with it, and that is not a fault in your phone. Do not buy a new TV for this. Ask a family member to check the TV's model with you, and to look up whether it supports screen mirroring.
  2. Your phone and your TV must be on the same Wi-Fi network. This is the requirement people forget most often. The phone and the TV talk to each other over Wi-Fi. If the phone is on your home Wi-Fi, and the TV is on the same home Wi-Fi, they can find each other. If the TV is on a different network, or if it is not connected to Wi-Fi at all, they cannot. To check the phone, read How to Connect Your Galaxy to Wi-Fi, and note the name of the Wi-Fi network. Then check the TV: open the TV's settings or network menu, and look at the name of the Wi-Fi network it is connected to. The two names must match, exactly. If they do not match, connect the TV to the same network as the phone, and try again. The TV's remote control, and its settings menu, are the way to do this. If the TV has no Wi-Fi at all, Smart View will not work with it.

If both requirements are true, you are ready. If you are not sure about the first one, try the steps below anyway. The worst that happens is that no TV appears in the list, and then you will know that one of the two requirements is not met. Trying costs nothing. The phone will not break, and the TV will not break.

Connect to the TV, step by step

Follow these steps in order. Take your time. Sit where you can see both the phone and the TV, so you can watch both screens.

  1. Turn on the TV. Make sure the TV is on, and showing its normal home screen, or a TV channel. It does not matter what the TV is showing, as long as it is on. Keep the TV remote control nearby, because the TV may ask you a question in a moment.
  2. On your phone, open the Quick settings panel. The Quick settings panel is the row of round buttons you see when you swipe down from the top of the screen. Swipe down once from the top of the screen. You should see a row of buttons, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and the torch. Swipe down a second time, to see the full panel, with more buttons.
  3. Look for the Smart View button. It may say "Smart View", or it may show a picture of a small screen with an arrow. It may not be on the first screen of buttons. Swipe the panel sideways, or look for a button that says "More", or an edit or pencil picture, to see the rest of the buttons. The exact place can be different on different phones, so look slowly. If you cannot find Smart View in the panel, read the "If the words on your screen look different" section at the end of this guide.
  4. Tap the Smart View button once. The phone should start looking for TVs nearby. This can take a few seconds. Wait, and do not tap again. A list should appear, with the names of the TVs the phone can see. Look for the name of your TV. It may be the name of the TV's maker, such as "Samsung TV", or a name someone gave the TV, such as "Living Room TV". Tap the name of your TV once.
  5. Watch the TV. The TV may show a message on its screen, asking if you want to allow the phone to connect. The message may say "Allow", or "Accept", or "Yes". Use the TV remote control to choose "Allow". If the TV shows a code, and the phone shows the same code, check that the two codes match, and then choose "Allow" on the TV. This is a safety step. It stops a stranger's phone from showing things on your TV.
  6. Wait a few seconds. The TV should now show a copy of your phone's screen. The phone's home screen, with its apps, should appear on the TV, big and clear. That is it. You are connected. The phone is mirroring to the TV.

Now try it with a photo, because that is the reason most people use Smart View. Open the Gallery app on your phone, and open a photo. The photo should appear on the TV, large enough for the whole room to see. Swipe to the next photo, and the TV should change too. When you have shown the photos, go back to the home screen on the phone, and the TV will show the home screen again. That is the whole job. A photo that was small on the phone is now big on the TV, and everyone can see it at the same time, without passing the phone around the room.

Stop mirroring, and what happens next

Stopping is as simple as starting. There are two ways, and both are easy.

  1. On the phone, swipe down from the top of the screen to open the Quick settings panel. Look for the Smart View button. It should look different now, because it is turned on: it may be coloured, or it may say "Stop" or "Disconnect". Tap it once. The TV should stop showing the phone's screen, and go back to what it was showing before, such as a TV channel or its home screen. The phone will stay as it was.
  2. If you cannot find the Smart View button, there is a simpler way. Lock the phone. Press the side button once to turn the phone's screen off. When the phone's screen turns off, the mirror turns off too. This is not the tidy way, but it always works, and it is easy to remember: screen off, mirror off.

After you stop, the phone and the TV remember each other. The next time you tap Smart View, your TV should appear in the list at once, and the TV may not ask you to allow the connection again, because you already allowed it once. If the TV asks again, that is normal too. Choose "Allow" again, and carry on.

One more thing to know. While you are mirroring, the phone's screen stays on. Do not be surprised if the phone's battery goes down faster than usual while you mirror. If you plan to show photos for a long time, it helps to plug the phone into its charger first. And if a private message arrives while you are mirroring, remember that the TV will show it too, because the TV is showing a copy of the whole screen. Stop mirroring for a moment, read the message on the phone, and then start again.

Try it slowly: one photo on the big screen

Let us practise with Ahmed and one photo. Ahmed has a photo of his family in the garden. He sits in his armchair, where he can see the TV. He turns the TV on. He checks that the phone and the TV are on the same Wi-Fi network. He swipes down from the top of the phone's screen, twice, to open the full Quick settings panel. He looks for the Smart View button, and he taps it. The phone looks for TVs. After a few seconds, one name appears: "Living Room TV". Ahmed taps it. The TV shows a message: "Allow this device?" Ahmed uses the TV remote to choose "Allow". A few seconds later, the TV shows his phone's home screen. Ahmed opens the Gallery, opens the garden photo, and the whole family sees it, big and clear, on the TV. Nobody has to pass the phone around. Nobody has to squint. Ahmed swipes to the next photo, and the TV changes too.

Now try the same practice yourself, with one photo that does not matter much. Connect to the TV. Open one photo. Check that the TV shows it. Swipe to the next photo, and check that the TV changes. Then stop mirroring, using the Smart View button, and check that the TV goes back to normal. Do this once, slowly, and you will understand Smart View better than any amount of reading. The next time, you will not need this guide at all.

If the TV does not appear, check these things first

Before you decide that Smart View is broken, check these six simple reasons. In most cases, one of them is the answer.

  1. The phone and the TV are on different Wi-Fi networks. This is the most common reason of all. Check the Wi-Fi name on the phone, and the Wi-Fi name on the TV. They must match, exactly. If they do not match, connect the TV to the same network as the phone. If the phone is using mobile data, and not Wi-Fi at all, Smart View will not find the TV. Connect the phone to Wi-Fi first.
  2. The TV is off, or it is not a smart TV. The TV must be turned on. An old TV with no smart features cannot do screen mirroring. If your TV is not a smart TV, Smart View will not work with it, and that is not a fault in your phone.
  3. The TV is too far away, or there is a wall in between. Smart View works over Wi-Fi, and Wi-Fi has a limited reach. If the phone is in one room and the TV is in another, or far down the house, the phone may not see the TV. Move closer to the TV, in the same room, and try again.
  4. The TV needs a moment. When you tap Smart View, the phone needs a few seconds to find the TV. If you tap again too quickly, or if you close the list too soon, you may miss it. Tap once, wait ten seconds, and watch the list. The TV often appears after a short wait.
  5. The TV is already connected to something else. If the TV is already showing a screen from another phone, or from a computer, it may not appear in your phone's list. Stop the other connection first, and then try again.
  6. The phone or the TV needs a restart. If everything else looks right, and the TV still does not appear, restart both. Turn the phone off and on again, and turn the TV off and on again. Then try the steps again. A restart fixes many small problems, and it costs nothing. Read How to Turn Your Samsung Galaxy On, Off, and Restart It if you are unsure how to restart the phone safely.

If none of these six reasons fits, ask a family member to look at the TV's settings with you, and to check whether the TV supports screen mirroring, and which Wi-Fi network it is on. A second pair of eyes often spots a different Wi-Fi name in seconds. Do not download an app that claims to connect your phone to the TV. The real Smart View is built into the phone, and it is reached through the Quick settings panel. An app that promises to do it for you is not needed, and it may be a scam.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to connect when the phone is on mobile data. Smart View needs Wi-Fi. If the phone is not on Wi-Fi, it will not find the TV. Connect the phone to your home Wi-Fi first.
  • Forgetting to check the TV's Wi-Fi name. The phone and the TV must be on the same network, with the same name. A TV connected to a neighbour's Wi-Fi, or to a different network in the house, will not appear in the list.
  • Thinking the TV is broken when the app blocks mirroring. Some video apps do not allow their video to be mirrored. The app may show a black screen, or a message, on the TV. That is the app's choice, not a fault in your phone or your TV. Try a photo from the Gallery instead, to check that Smart View itself works.
  • Forgetting that the TV shows private messages too. Smart View copies the whole screen. If a private message arrives while you are mirroring, everyone in the room will see it on the TV. Stop mirroring before you read a private message.
  • Letting the phone's screen turn off during a long show. If the phone's screen turns off, the mirror turns off too. Keep the phone's screen on while you mirror, and plug the phone into its charger if you plan to show things for a long time.
  • Standing too far from the TV. Smart View works best in the same room as the TV. If you are far away, or behind a wall, the connection may fail, or the picture may freeze. Sit in the same room as the TV.
  • Believing an app that promises to connect the phone to the TV. Smart View is built into the phone. You do not need an app for it. An app that claims to do it for you is not needed, and it may be a scam. Use the Quick settings panel.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a cable to connect my phone to the TV?

No. Smart View works without a cable. It uses Wi-Fi to send the phone's screen to the TV. You do not need to buy a cable, and you do not need to plug anything into the phone or the TV.

Can I use the phone for other things while it is mirroring?

You can, but the TV will show whatever you do on the phone, because the TV is showing a copy of the whole screen. If you open a message, the TV shows the message. If you want to use the phone privately while the family watches the TV, stop mirroring first, and then use the phone.

Why does the TV show a black screen when I play a video in an app?

Some video apps do not allow their video to be mirrored. They show a black screen, or a message, on the TV, while the sound may still play. This is the app's choice, usually to protect its video. It is not a fault in your phone or your TV. Try a photo or a video from the Gallery, which should mirror normally, to check that Smart View itself works.

Will the phone's battery run down faster while mirroring?

Yes, a little faster than usual, because the phone is working harder to show its screen on the TV. For a few photos, this does not matter. For a long video, plug the phone into its charger first, so it does not run out of battery in the middle.

How do I know if the phone and the TV are on the same Wi-Fi?

Check the Wi-Fi name on the phone, in Settings, or in the Quick settings panel, and check the Wi-Fi name on the TV, in the TV's settings or network menu. The two names must match, exactly. If they do not match, connect the TV to the same network as the phone.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words may look a little different on your phone. That is normal. Samsung makes many Galaxy models. Some are A-series phones. Some are S-series phones. They do not all show the exact same words. Your phone company, called a carrier, can also change some words. Your country or region can change words too. And your One UI version matters. In October 2026, some phones use One UI 8, some use One UI 8.5, and some use One UI 9, depending on the model and the region. No one version is "the" version for every phone. That is why this guide tells you to look for the words "Smart View" in the Quick settings panel, and to swipe the panel sideways, or look for the edit or pencil picture, to see all the buttons.

If you cannot find Smart View in the Quick settings panel at all, check two things. First, check that you swiped down twice, to see the full panel, and that you swiped the panel sideways to see the rest of the buttons. Second, check that your phone is a Samsung Galaxy phone. Smart View is a Samsung feature. It does not exist on other brands. If your phone is a Galaxy and the button is still nowhere to be found, your phone may need a software update, or the button may be hidden and need to be added through the panel's edit option. Look for a pencil picture, or the word "Edit", in the panel, and look for Smart View in the list of buttons you can add. Do not download an app that claims to be Smart View. The real one is built into the phone, and it is reached through the Quick settings panel.
