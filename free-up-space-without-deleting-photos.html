How to Free Up Space on Your Galaxy Without Deleting Your Photos

Harold is 74 years old. He loves taking photos of his grandchildren. He takes photos at birthdays. He takes photos in the garden. He takes photos of his dog, Milo, sleeping in the sun. His phone now says Storage almost full. Harold feels worried. He thinks he must delete his photos. He does not want to delete them. Each photo is a memory. He is right to pause. You do not need to delete your photos first. There are safer ways to free up space. This guide shows you those ways, one step at a time.

Storage is the space inside your phone. Think of it like a cupboard. Photos, videos, apps, and files all live in that cupboard. When the cupboard is full, the phone gets slow. New photos may not save. Apps may not update. The phone may warn you again and again. Freeing up space means making room in the cupboard. You can remove things you do not need. You can clean up hidden clutter. You can keep the things that matter most. Your photos can stay. That is the promise of this guide.

Before you start, do not delete anything in a rush. Do not tap Delete all. Do not empty any folder until you know what is inside it. Sit down with a cup of tea. Make sure your phone has some battery left. You will check how much space is used first. Then you will clean up in small, safe steps. If you are unsure about a file, leave it alone. Ask a family member before you delete it. A slow, careful clean is better than a fast clean that removes something precious.

First, check how much space you have

It helps to see what is using the most space. Is it videos? Is it apps? Is it downloads? Once you know, you can clean the right place. Do not guess. Look first.

  1. Open Settings. Look for the gear icon. Tap it once.
  2. Tap Device care. On some phones the words are Battery and device care. Tap those words if you see them instead. The meaning is the same.
  3. Tap Storage. You will see a bar or a list. It shows how much space is used and how much is free. It may show Photos, Videos, Apps, and Other files. Read the numbers slowly. Note which group is biggest.
  4. Do not tap anything yet. Just look. If Videos use the most space, your videos are the best place to start. If Apps use the most space, your apps are the best place to start. Write the biggest group on a piece of paper if that helps you remember.

Steps to free up space without deleting photos

  1. In Storage, look for a clean-up or optimise option if your phone shows one. Some phones show a button that suggests files to remove, such as old temporary files. Read each suggestion carefully before you accept it. Only accept suggestions about temporary files or cache. Do not accept a suggestion to delete photos or videos.
  2. Clear app cache, one app at a time. Cache is temporary data that apps store to work faster. It builds up over time. It is usually safe to clear. In Device care, tap Storage, then look for Apps. Tap an app you use a lot, such as a web browser or a video app. Look for Storage inside the app details. Look for the words Clear cache. Tap Clear cache. Do NOT tap Clear data. Clear data can remove your logins and settings for that app. Clear cache is the safe choice. Repeat this for one or two large apps only. You do not need to do every app today.
  3. Delete old downloads you no longer need. Open your Files app. It may be called My Files. Look for Downloads. Open it. You may see old documents, old tickets, or old files you saved once and forgot. Tap one file to open it and check what it is. If you are sure you do not need it, delete only that one file. Do this slowly, one file at a time. Leave anything you are unsure about.
  4. Remove apps you never use. Go to Settings, then Apps. Scroll through the list. Ask yourself: Have I used this app in the last three months? Do I know what it does? If the answer is no to both, you may not need it. Tap the app. Tap Uninstall. Only uninstall apps you chose yourself, such as games or shopping apps. Do not uninstall apps with names you do not recognise that came with the phone. Those may be needed for the phone to work. If in doubt, leave the app alone and ask a family member.
  5. Check your videos. Videos use much more space than photos. One long video can use as much space as hundreds of photos. Open your Gallery. Look at your videos. Find a video you have already shared with family, or a video that is blurry and means little to you. If you are sure you do not need it, delete only that video. Keep the videos that matter. Do not delete videos in bulk. One by one is safer.
  6. Check the trash or recycle bin in your Gallery. When you delete a photo or video, it often goes to a trash folder first. It stays there for a while before it is gone for good. That trash folder still uses space. Open your Gallery. Look for the menu, often three lines or three dots. Look for Trash or Recycle bin. Open it. If you see photos you deleted on purpose and you are sure you do not want them back, you can empty the trash. But check each item first. If you see a photo you want to keep, restore it before you empty the trash. This step alone can free up a lot of space.
  7. Restart your phone. Hold the side button until the power menu appears. Tap Restart. Wait for the phone to turn back on. A restart can clear temporary clutter. It is a simple step. Do it after your clean-up, not during it.

A small practice: Harold and the garden videos

Harold sat at his kitchen table on a Sunday morning. His granddaughter Lily was visiting. Lily asked him to open Settings. Harold tapped Device care. He tapped Storage. Videos were the biggest group. Harold felt a little shocked. He had filmed Milo the dog many times. Some videos were five minutes long. Some were almost the same. Lily helped him open the Gallery. They watched each video together. They kept the video of Milo chasing a ball. They kept the video of the grandchildren blowing out birthday candles. They deleted three long, shaky videos of the garden fence. Harold did not delete a single photo.

Then Lily showed Harold the trash folder. There were old screenshots Harold had deleted months ago. They were still using space. Harold checked them one by one. He was sure he did not need them. He emptied the trash. Next, Harold cleared the cache for his web browser. He did not tap Clear data. He tapped Clear cache only. After a restart, his phone showed much more free space. The warning went away. Harold kept all his grandchild photos. He learned a simple rule. Check storage first. Clean videos and trash first. Delete photos last, and only if you truly choose to. You can follow Harold. Do one step. Then stop. Then do the next step another day if you feel tired.

Common mistakes

  • Deleting all photos in a panic when the storage warning appears, instead of checking what is really using the space, and losing memories that cannot be recovered.
  • Tapping Clear data instead of Clear cache in an app, which can sign you out and remove app settings, when Clear cache alone would have freed space safely.
  • Emptying the Gallery trash without checking what is inside, and losing a photo that was deleted by accident and could still have been restored.
  • Uninstalling an app that came with the phone because its name looked strange, which can stop a phone feature from working, instead of only removing apps you installed yourself.
  • Deleting a video that is the only copy of a family moment, because it was large, without first asking family if they have a copy or checking if it was already shared and saved elsewhere.

Frequently asked questions

Will clearing cache delete my photos or messages?

No. Clearing cache removes temporary files only. Your photos, messages, and contacts stay. You may need to wait a moment the next time an app opens, because it rebuilds its temporary files. That is normal.

How much free space should I aim for?

Aim for at least a few gigabytes free if you can. Your phone works better when it is not almost full. You do not need to empty half the phone. Even freeing a small amount can stop the warnings and let you take new photos.

What if I already deleted a photo by mistake?

Check the trash or recycle bin in your Gallery straight away. Deleted photos often stay there for a set time. If the photo is there, tap it and tap Restore. If it is not there, ask a family member if they have a copy. Do not download a recovery app in a panic. Many of those apps ask for money or access you should not give.

Should I move my photos to a memory card?

Only if your phone has a memory card slot and you know how to use it safely. Not all Galaxy phones take a memory card. If you are unsure, do not buy a card yet. Ask a family member or a trusted shop to check your model first. Moving photos the wrong way can make them hard to find later.

Why does my phone fill up again so fast?

Videos, app updates, and messages with photos and videos fill space quickly. Group chats can be a big cause. Check your message apps from time to time. Delete old videos in chats that you have already saved or do not need. Check storage once a month. Small, regular cleans are easier than one big clean.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words may differ by model, region, carrier, and One UI version. One UI 8, 8.5, and 9 all exist in October 2026. Some phones say Device care. Others say Battery and device care. Both lead to the same place. The words Storage, Clear cache, Trash, and Recycle bin are the words to look for. If you cannot find a word, open Settings and use the search box at the top. Type Storage or Clear cache. Tap the result that matches. If a screen asks you to confirm a deletion and you are unsure, tap Cancel. Leave that file alone. Ask for help before you try again. Going slow keeps your photos safe.
