Simple Phone Help for Seniors with a Samsung Galaxy

This site is for older adults and complete beginners who use a Samsung Galaxy phone. Every guide is written in plain, simple English. There is no jargon here, and no step assumes you already know your way around a phone.

We cover only Samsung Galaxy phones running One UI. That is the menu system Samsung puts on its Galaxy phones. We cover the everyday things most people actually need: making calls, sending photos, using WhatsApp, changing text size, connecting to Wi-Fi, saving contacts, setting alarms, and keeping the phone charged and working well.

Steps are written one at a time, in the order you tap them. If a menu word on your phone looks a little different from the word in a guide, do not worry. Menu words can differ slightly by phone model, region, phone company, and One UI version. When that happens, open Settings, tap the search box at the top, and type the name of the setting you are looking for. That search box is the easiest way to find almost anything on a Galaxy phone.

Nothing on this site needs an account, a download, or a payment. The tools on this site run on your own phone or computer, in your browser. Your information is not sent anywhere by this site.

Start here if you are new

If you have just got a Galaxy phone, or you are helping someone who has, start with these three steps. Take them slowly. There is no rush.

  1. Make the text comfortable first. Read How to Make Text Bigger on Your Samsung Galaxy and set the text to a size you can read without straining. Everything else gets easier after this.
  2. Save the people who matter. Read How to Save a Contact and Find It Again on Your Galaxy, then save family and close friends. After that, read How to Set Up Emergency Contacts and SOS on Your Galaxy and add emergency contacts too.
  3. Learn the safety basics. Read How to Spot a Scam Call or Message on Your Galaxy and How to Block and Silence Unknown Callers on Your Galaxy. Most phone trouble for beginners starts with a strange call or message, and these two guides help you stay calm and safe.

A note about safety

Stop and check before you act. A real bank, a real phone company, and a real government office will not rush you on a phone call, will not ask you for passwords or one-time codes, and will not ask you to move money to stay safe. If a call or message pressures you, asks for money or codes, or says your account will be closed today, stop. Hang up, or do not reply. You can always contact the company yourself, using a number you already trust, and ask if the message was real.

If you already shared money details or codes, contact your bank directly straight away, using the number on your bank card or bank statement. Do not use a number from the suspicious call or message. For more help, use the Scam Call/Message Checker and read What to Do If You Tapped a Suspicious Link on Your Galaxy.
