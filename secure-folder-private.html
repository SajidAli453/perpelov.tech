How to Use Secure Folder on Your Samsung Galaxy to Keep Private Photos and Apps Locked

Rashid is 71 years old. He uses his Samsung Galaxy phone every day. He takes photos of his grandchildren. He keeps photos of old family letters. He also keeps a note with family birthdays, and a copy of a letter about a doctor's appointment. One day, his grandson asks to borrow the phone to play a game. Rashid hands it over with a smile. Then he worries. The grandson is a good boy, and he will only play the game. But a child taps everywhere. A finger can slip. A photo can open by mistake. A note can be deleted by mistake. Rashid takes the phone back, politely, sooner than he wanted to. He does not want to say no to his grandson. He wants a place on his phone where private things can sit behind a lock, safe from curious fingers, while the rest of the phone stays open and friendly for everyone.

That locked place already exists on your Galaxy phone. It is called Secure Folder. Think of it as a small locked drawer inside your phone. The phone itself is the house. Everyone in the family can walk around the house. The drawer stays locked. Inside the drawer, you can keep photos, files, notes, and even whole apps, separate from the rest of the phone. Photos moved into Secure Folder do not show in your normal Gallery. Apps added to Secure Folder live as a separate copy, with their own sign-in, inside the locked drawer. When the folder is locked, nobody can see what is inside it without the lock. When you unlock it, you can use everything inside as normal. When you lock it again, it is safe again.

Secure Folder is not a second phone, and it is not a hiding place for breaking rules. It is a normal Samsung feature, made for this everyday need: some things are private, and private things deserve a lock. This guide teaches you how to set it up, how to move photos into it, how to add apps to it, how to open it and lock it, and what to do if you forget the lock. Read the whole guide once before you start, especially the part about forgetting the lock. A lock that is forgotten can keep you out of your own drawer. This guide will help you avoid that.

Before you start, you need three things. First, your phone, charged enough to stay on for about fifteen minutes, because setting up the folder takes a little while. Second, your Samsung account details. A Samsung account is the free account Samsung asks you to make when you first set up a Galaxy phone. Secure Folder asks you to sign in with it. If you do not know your Samsung account details, stop here and ask a family member to help you find them before you go on. Do not guess a password. Do not use someone else's account. The folder is tied to your account, and that is what makes recovery possible if you forget the lock. Third, a lock you will remember: a PIN, a pattern, a password, or the fingerprint you already use. You will choose it during setup.

Set up Secure Folder, step by step

Follow these steps in order. Take your time. The exact place where Secure Folder lives in Settings can be different on different phones, so this guide tells you to search for it, rather than sending you down one fixed path that may not match your phone. Searching is the reliable way, on every Galaxy phone.

  1. Open Settings. Settings is the app with a picture that looks like a small gear, or cog wheel. Find it in your apps list and tap it once.
  2. Look at the top of the Settings screen. You should see a search box, with words like "Search" or a small magnifying glass picture. Tap the search box. A keyboard should appear.
  3. Type the words "Secure Folder". Type slowly, and watch the screen. Before you finish typing, a list of results should appear. Look for a result that says "Secure Folder". It may have a small picture of a folder with a lock. Tap that result once.
  4. The Secure Folder screen should open, with a short explanation of what Secure Folder is. Read it slowly. When you are ready, look for a button that says something like "Continue", "Set up", or "Agree". Tap it.
  5. The phone may ask you to sign in with your Samsung account. Type your Samsung account email address and password carefully. Do this alone, or with a family member you trust beside you. Never tell your password to a stranger on the phone, and never type it because a message told you to. If you are signed in already, the phone may skip this step. That is normal.
  6. Next, the phone will ask you to choose a lock type for the folder: a PIN (a short number), a pattern (a shape drawn with your finger), a password, or a fingerprint, if your phone has fingerprint unlocking. Choose the lock type that feels easiest for you. Many people choose a PIN, because it is quick to type and easy to remember. If you choose a fingerprint, the phone will still ask you to set a PIN or pattern as a backup. Follow the words on the screen. Type your lock twice, slowly, when the phone asks you to confirm it. The two entries must match.
  7. Wait while the phone creates the folder. This can take a minute or two. Do not close Settings. Do not turn the phone off. When it finishes, the phone should show you the inside of your new Secure Folder: a small, mostly empty apps screen, with a few apps already inside, such as the Gallery, the Camera, and a files app. Your folder is ready.

Before you go on, do one important thing. Write your lock down on a piece of paper, and keep that paper somewhere safe in your home, such as inside a drawer at home. Do not write it on your phone. Do not keep it in a note on the phone. Do not take a photo of it. If you forget the lock one day, that paper note will save you. If you do not make the note, and you forget the lock, you may not be able to open the folder at all. Make the note now, while the lock is fresh in your mind.

Move photos into Secure Folder

Moving a photo into Secure Folder is like moving a letter from a shelf into the locked drawer. The word to remember is "move". When you move a photo, it leaves your normal Gallery. It no longer shows there. It only shows inside Secure Folder. That is what you want for a private photo. Practise with one photo first, not with fifty at once. Move one photo. Check that it has gone from the normal Gallery. Check that it is inside the folder. Then move the rest, a few at a time.

  1. Open the Gallery app, the normal one, from your home screen or apps list. Find the photo you want to move. Do not open it yet. Look at it in the grid of small pictures.
  2. Press and hold the photo. Put your finger on the photo and keep it there, without lifting, for about two seconds. The photo should become selected, with a tick mark on it or a coloured border around it.
  3. If you want to move more than one photo, tap the other photos one by one while the first is still selected. Each one should get a tick mark too. For your first try, select only one photo.
  4. Look for a menu button: three small dots in a corner of the screen, or the word "More". Tap it. A small menu should appear.
  5. Read the menu slowly. Look for the words "Move to Secure Folder". Tap those words. The phone may ask you to confirm, saying the photo will no longer appear in the normal Gallery. That is what you want. Tap the confirm button, which may say "Move" or "OK".
  6. Wait a moment. The photo should disappear from the normal Gallery. Do not panic. It has not been deleted. To check, open Secure Folder, unlock it, and open the Gallery app inside the folder. Your photo should be there.

Add apps to Secure Folder

When you add an app to Secure Folder, the phone makes a second, separate copy of that app, inside the locked drawer. The copy inside the folder is separate from the copy outside it. It has its own sign-in, and it keeps its own photos, messages, and files. This is useful if you want a private copy of an app, such as a notes app, while the normal copy stays open for everyday use. You can keep everyday notes outside, and private notes inside, in the copy behind the lock.

  1. Open Secure Folder. Find the Secure Folder app on your home screen or in your apps list. Tap it, and unlock it with your lock.
  2. Look for a button to add apps. It may say "Add apps", or it may show a plus sign, a + mark. Tap it.
  3. A list of your apps should appear, with a small empty circle next to each app name. Tap the circle next to each app you want to add. A tick mark should appear. For your first try, add only one app, such as the Gallery app or a notes app.
  4. Look for a button that says "Add", or "Done", or a tick mark at the top of the screen. Tap it. Wait while the phone makes the separate copy. The app should now appear on the Secure Folder apps screen.
  5. Tap the new copy of the app inside the folder. It will open as if it were brand new. If it needs a sign-in, it will ask you to sign in again, inside the folder. That is normal. The inside copy does not share the sign-in of the outside copy. Do this alone, or with a family member you trust beside you. Never share a password with a stranger.

Open the folder, use it, and lock it again

Opening Secure Folder is simple. Find the Secure Folder app and tap it. Use your lock. The folder opens. Use the apps and photos inside it as normal. Everything inside works the same way as outside. The only difference is the lock on the door. While the folder is open, it stays open until you lock it, or until the phone locks itself. If you hand the phone to someone while the folder is open, they can see inside it. So lock the folder before you hand the phone to anyone. An unlocked drawer is not a locked drawer.

To lock the folder, look for the folder menu: three small dots, or the word "More", in a corner of the screen. Tap it, look for the word "Lock", and tap it. The folder should close and lock at once. If you cannot find a Lock word, lock the whole phone instead: press the side button once to turn the screen off. When the phone locks, the folder locks too.

Try it slowly: one photo, locked away

Let us practise with Rashid and one photo. Rashid has a photo of an old family letter. It is not a secret. It is simply private. He opens his normal Gallery, finds the photo, and presses and holds it until it is selected. He taps the three dots, reads the menu slowly, finds "Move to Secure Folder", taps it, and confirms. The photo disappears from his normal Gallery. Rashid does not panic. He expected that. He opens Secure Folder, uses his PIN, and opens the Gallery app inside the folder. There is the photo, safe inside the locked drawer. He locks the folder again.

Now try the same practice yourself, with one photo that does not matter much. Move it into the folder. Check that it is gone from the normal Gallery. Check that it is inside the folder. Lock the folder. Ask a family member to look for the photo in the normal Gallery. They should not find it. Then move the photo back out, if you want it in the normal Gallery again. Do this once, slowly, and you will understand Secure Folder better than any amount of reading.

If you forget the lock, read this first

This is the most important part of this guide. Please read it slowly. Secure Folder is protected by your lock and by your Samsung account. If you forget the lock, the phone will ask you to try again. After a few wrong tries, the phone may offer a way to reset the lock using your Samsung account. Look for words on the lock screen about forgetting the lock, or about resetting it with your Samsung account. If those words appear, follow them, and sign in with your Samsung account when asked. That is the safe, proper way back in. It only works if you know your Samsung account details, and if the phone can reach the internet. That is why setup asked for your Samsung account. It is your spare key.

If you forget the lock, and you cannot sign in to your Samsung account either, the folder may stay locked. This is hard news, but it is honest news. Samsung, and this guide, cannot promise a way to open a locked folder without the lock or the account. There is no secret back door. Anyone who tells you they can open it for money, or asks you to install an app to open it, is not helping you. Do not pay them. Do not install their app. Read How to Spot a Scam Call or Message on Your Galaxy before you believe any message that offers to unlock your phone.

The way to avoid this trouble is simple: write your lock down on paper, and keep that paper safe at home. Also keep your Samsung account details written down in the same safe place at home, on paper, not on the phone. And keep copies of truly important photos and papers outside the folder too. If a photo matters so much that losing it would upset you deeply, keep a copy outside the folder, or print it, or share a copy with a family member you trust.

If you have forgotten the lock and the account, do not keep guessing the lock again and again. Guessing too many times can lock the folder for longer. Stop. Take the phone to a Samsung service centre or a trusted repair shop, and tell them exactly what happened. They may be able to help you with the account, even if they cannot open the folder itself. Do not feel foolish. Forgetting a lock happens to many people. The paper note at home is how wise people avoid it next time.

Common mistakes

  • Not writing the lock down on paper. A lock kept only in your head can be forgotten. Write it on paper, keep the paper safe at home, and do it on the day you set up the folder, not "later".
  • Sharing the lock or a code with a stranger. Nobody from Samsung, your bank, or your phone company will ever call or message you to ask for your Secure Folder lock, your Samsung account password, or a code. If anyone asks, it is a scam. Hang up and tell a family member.
  • Thinking a moved photo was deleted. When you move a photo into Secure Folder, it disappears from the normal Gallery. That is supposed to happen. Open the folder and look in the Gallery app inside it. The photo should be there.
  • Handing the phone to someone while the folder is still open. An open folder is not a locked folder. Lock the folder, or lock the whole phone, before you hand the phone to anyone. It takes two seconds.
  • Keeping the only copy of a precious photo inside the folder. The folder is a private drawer, not a backup. Keep a copy of precious photos outside the folder too, or printed, or shared with a family member you trust.
  • Hiding the folder icon and forgetting where it went. Hiding is optional and confuses many people. To open a hidden folder, search Settings for "Secure Folder". If that sounds like too much to remember, do not hide the icon.
  • Using a lock anyone can guess. A PIN like your birth year is easy for someone who knows you to guess. Choose a lock that is easy for you to remember but hard for someone else to guess, and do not use the same lock for the folder that you use for your bank or email, if you can avoid it.
  • Believing a message that offers to unlock the folder. There is no app or paid service that can honestly unlock a forgotten Secure Folder lock. Messages that offer this are scams. Use Samsung account recovery, or take the phone to a Samsung service centre.

Frequently asked questions

Can anyone see my photos inside Secure Folder without the lock?

No. When the folder is locked, its photos do not show in the normal Gallery, and its apps do not show their contents outside the folder. Someone holding your locked phone cannot open the folder without the lock. The protection only works while the folder is locked, so lock it before you hand the phone to anyone.

Do I need the internet to use Secure Folder?

No, not for everyday use. Once the folder is set up, you can open it, move photos, and use apps inside it without Wi-Fi or mobile data. The internet is only needed during setup, when you sign in with your Samsung account, and if you ever need to reset a forgotten lock using your Samsung account.

Can I move a photo back out of Secure Folder?

Yes. Open the Gallery app inside the folder, press and hold the photo, open the menu, and look for words about moving it out of Secure Folder. Tap them, and confirm. The photo will appear in your normal Gallery again. Always check afterwards that the photo is where you expected it to be.

What if I forget my Samsung account password too?

Recover the account first. On the sign-in screen, look for words about forgetting the password, and follow them. The phone will usually offer to send a reset link or code to your email address. Do this with a family member beside you, and take your time. Never share the reset code with anyone who calls or messages you about it. Once the account is recovered, try the folder lock reset again. And write both the lock and the account details on paper, at home.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words may look a little different on your phone. That is normal. Samsung makes many Galaxy models. Some are A-series phones. Some are S-series phones. They do not all show the exact same words. Your phone company, called a carrier, can also change some words. Your country or region can change words too. And your One UI version matters. In October 2026, some phones use One UI 8, some use One UI 8.5, and some use One UI 9, depending on the model and the region. No one version is "the" version for every phone. On one phone, Secure Folder may sit in one part of Settings. On another, it may sit in a different part. That is why this guide tells you to search for "Secure Folder" in the Settings search box, instead of sending you down one fixed path. The search box works on every Galaxy phone, on every One UI version. Use it.

If you cannot find Secure Folder in the search results, check two things. First, check that you spelled it as two words, "Secure Folder", with a space in the middle. Second, check that your phone is a Samsung Galaxy phone. Secure Folder is a Samsung feature. It does not exist on other brands. If your phone is a Galaxy and the search still shows nothing, your phone may need a software update, or the feature may be named a little differently in your region. Ask a family member to look at the search with you, or take the phone to a Samsung service centre. Do not download an app that claims to be Secure Folder. The real one is built into the phone, and it is reached through Settings.
