How to Use Two Apps at Once on Your Samsung Galaxy (Split Screen and Pop-Up View)

Amina is 68 years old. One afternoon, her grandson sends her a short video of his school play. She opens the video and watches it with a smile. Halfway through, her daughter sends her a message: "Mum, what time is the family dinner on Sunday?" Amina wants to answer, but she does not want to stop the video. She taps the message. The video disappears. She answers her daughter. Then she has to find the video again, open it again, and watch from the start. She does this three times in one afternoon. By the third time, she is a little annoyed with her phone. She thinks the phone can only show one thing at a time. It cannot. It can show two.

Your Samsung Galaxy phone can show two apps on the screen at the same time. This is called split screen. One app takes the top half of the screen. The other app takes the bottom half. Both apps stay open. Both stay on show. You can watch the video on the top half and write your message on the bottom half. You can read a message on the top half and tap a phone number into the Phone app on the bottom half. You do not have to jump back and forth. You do not have to remember what you just read. Both things are in front of your eyes, at the same time.

There is a second way, called pop-up view. In pop-up view, one app stays big, filling the whole screen, and the second app floats on top of it in a small window, like a small note stuck on a page. You can move the small window around, make it bigger or smaller, and close it when you are done. Pop-up view is good when you only need a quick look at the second app, or a quick reply, and you want the first app to stay big and easy to see. This guide teaches both ways, slowly, one step at a time. Nothing here needs a download or a payment. Both ways are built into your phone already.

Before you start, you need two apps you want to use together. A video app and a messaging app are a good pair. A message and the Phone app are another good pair. Your phone should be charged enough to stay on while you learn. The words on the screen will be smaller when two apps share the screen, because each app only gets half the space. That is normal. If the words look too small, make the text bigger first by reading How to Make Text Bigger on Your Samsung Galaxy, then come back here. Take your time. Nothing here can break your phone.

Open two apps side by side, step by step

Follow these steps in order. Take your time. Look at the words and pictures on your screen as you go.

  1. Open the first app you want to use. For practice, open your messaging app, or open a video. The first app is the one that will sit on the top half of the screen when you are finished.
  2. Open the list of apps you have used lately. This list is called Recents. If your phone has three buttons at the bottom of the screen, tap the button on the left. It usually looks like three short lines, or a small square. If your phone has no buttons and you move around by swiping, swipe up from the bottom of the screen and hold your finger still in the middle of the screen for a second. The Recents list should appear either way, showing your open apps as a row of cards.
  3. Find the card for the app you just opened. Look at the top of that card. In the middle of the top edge, you should see the small picture, or icon, for the app. Tap that icon once. A small menu should appear. Do not tap the middle of the card. Tap the small icon at the top. If nothing happens, tap the icon again, slowly, and wait one second.
  4. Read the menu. Look for the words "Open in split screen view". The exact words may be a little different on your phone, but they will say something about split screen. Tap those words. The first app should move to the top half of the screen. The bottom half should show a list of your other apps, waiting for you to choose the second app.
  5. Choose the second app. Tap the app you want on the bottom half, for example the Phone app or your messaging app. It should fill the bottom half. Now both apps are on show. In the middle, between the two halves, there is a thin bar. That bar is the divider. It marks the line where one app ends and the other begins.
  6. Try using both apps. Tap the top half, and use the first app as normal. Tap the bottom half, and use the second app as normal. Only one half is active at a time, the half you last tapped. The other half waits quietly. If you tap a button and nothing happens, check which half you tapped. Tap the half you want to use first, then tap its buttons.
  7. When you are finished, close the split. Drag the divider bar slowly all the way to the top edge of the screen, or all the way to the bottom edge. The app on the side you drag towards will close, and the other app will fill the whole screen again. Or tap the divider bar once, gently, and look for a close mark, a small X, in the small options that appear, and tap it. Either way, the app you keep stays open, full screen.

That is split screen. Open an app, open Recents, tap the app icon at the top of its card, choose split screen view, and pick the second app. It sounds like a lot of steps the first time. After three or four tries, your fingers will know the way. Learn it by doing it for a real job, not by practising for its own sake: a video and a message, or a message and the Phone app. A real job gives your fingers a reason to remember.

Change the sizes, swap the halves, and close one app

Split screen does not have to be half and half. You can give one app more room and the other less. A video is nicer when it is bigger. A message is fine when it is smaller. Put your finger on the divider bar in the middle of the screen. Drag it slowly up to make the bottom app bigger, or drag it slowly down to make the top app bigger. Let go when the sizes feel right. Drag slowly. A slow drag gives the phone time to follow your finger, and gives your eyes time to see when the size is right.

You can also swap the two halves, so the app on top moves to the bottom and the app on the bottom moves to the top. Tap the divider bar once, gently. A small set of options should appear. Look for words about swapping, or two arrows pointing at each other. Tap that option. The apps should trade places. If you cannot find a swap option on your phone, do not worry. Some phones show it and some do not. You can get the same result by closing the split and opening it again with the apps in the other order.

To close one app but keep the other, drag the divider all the way to the edge of the app you want to close. Dragging to the top edge closes the top app. Dragging to the bottom edge closes the bottom app. Drag slowly, and watch which app is getting smaller. If the wrong app is shrinking, let go and drag the other way. Nothing is lost by letting go. The split simply stays as it was, and you can try again.

Open one app in a small floating window

Pop-up view is best when the second app is only needed for a moment. Imagine you are reading a long article, and you want to check one short message. You do not want to split the screen in half for one short message. You want a small window that floats over the article, that you can answer and then close. That is pop-up view. The main app stays big behind it. When you close the small window, the main app is still there, exactly as you left it.

The steps are almost the same as for split screen. The only difference is the choice you make in the small menu.

  1. Open the first app, the one you want to stay big. This app will fill the screen behind the small window.
  2. Open the Recents list, the same way as before: the Recents button, or swipe up and hold.
  3. Find the card for the second app, the app you want in the small window. If it is not open yet, open it first, then come back to the Recents list. The card must be in the list before you can turn it into a small window.
  4. Tap the small app icon at the top of that card. In the menu, look for the words "Open in pop-up view". Tap those words. The app should shrink into a small floating window on top of your first app.
  5. Try the small window. Tap inside it and use it as normal. Tap outside it, on the big app, and the big app becomes active again. The small window stays where you put it, waiting. The window you last tapped is the one that takes your taps.

To move the window, put your finger on the thin bar at the very top of the small window and drag it slowly to a new place. To make the window bigger or smaller, drag from a corner of the window, out to make it bigger, in to make it smaller. If one corner does nothing, try the opposite corner. To close the window, look at the top of the window for a small X and tap it. If you cannot see an X, tap the thin bar at the top once and look for a close mark in the options that appear. Some phones also let you shrink the window into a tiny floating icon, by tapping a small dash mark at the top. If you do not see a dash mark, your phone may not offer that choice. Closing the window is always enough.

Try it slowly: the phone number from a message

Let us practise with Amina and a real job. Her daughter sends her a message: "Mum, call the plumber on 0161 496 0234. He can come on Thursday." Amina needs to call that number. In the past, she would read the number, try to remember it, open the Phone app, and tap the number in from memory. Sometimes she got a digit wrong. Split screen takes the remembering away. Both the message and the Phone app are on show at once. She can look at the number while she taps it in.

Amina opens the message first, so she can read the number. She opens the Recents list, finds the message card, taps the small icon at the top of the card, and taps "Open in split screen view". The message moves to the top half. She taps the Phone app for the bottom half. The number, 0161 496 0234, is in front of her eyes on the top half. She taps the bottom half, so the Phone app is active, and taps the number keys one digit at a time, reading each digit from the message as she goes. When the number is complete, she reads it once more, slowly, and checks it against the message. Then she taps the call button. The job is done, with no remembering and no guessing.

Now try it yourself, with a number that does not matter. Ask a family member to send you a message with a made-up number, or use a number from an old message that you do not need to call. Open the message, open split screen, open the Phone app, and tap the number in, digit by digit, reading from the message. Do not tap the call button. Tap the clear button to erase the number instead. Do it three times. The first time, go slowly and read every step. The second time, try without reading the steps. The third time, put the message on the bottom half and the Phone app on the top half, so you learn that the order does not matter.

If you want an even easier way to move a number, read How to Copy and Paste Text on Your Samsung Galaxy. Copy and paste moves a number from a message into the Phone app without typing it at all. Split screen and copy and paste work well together: split screen lets you see both apps, and copy and paste moves the number. Learn both ways, and use whichever feels easier on the day.

When split screen or pop-up view does not work

Sometimes split screen does not work, even when you follow the steps exactly. This is usually a small, simple reason. Work through this short list, one item at a time.

First, the app itself may not allow split screen. Not every app can share the screen. Some apps, often games, will not offer the split screen choice at all. The menu may simply not show the words "Open in split screen view", or it may show them in a pale colour, which means they cannot be tapped. That is not a fault in your phone. It is a choice made by the app. Try a different second app. Messaging apps, the Phone app, the browser, and the Gallery usually work well.

Second, the app may not be open yet. The Recents list only shows apps you have opened. If the second app is not in the list, open it first in the normal way, from your home screen, then open the Recents list again. The card should now be there.

Third, restart your phone. A restart can clear a small software hiccup that stops the menu from appearing. Turning a Galaxy phone off and on again is explained in the guide called How to Turn Your Samsung Galaxy On, Off, and Restart It. After the restart, try again. If split screen still does not work for any app, ask a family member to look at it with you, or take the phone to a Samsung service centre. Tell them exactly what you tried, and which apps you tried.

Common mistakes

  • Tapping the middle of the card instead of the small icon at the top. The menu only appears when you tap the small app icon at the top edge of the card in the Recents list. Tapping the middle of the card simply opens the app full screen.
  • Tapping buttons in the half that is waiting. Only the half you last tapped is active. Tap the half you want to use first, then tap its buttons.
  • Dragging the divider too fast. A fast drag can close the wrong app. Drag slowly, watch the sizes change, and let go as soon as they feel right.
  • Closing the wrong app with the divider. Dragging to the top edge closes the top app. Dragging to the bottom edge closes the bottom app. Watch which app is shrinking, and let go if it is the wrong one.
  • Expecting every app to work in split screen. Some apps do not allow it. That is the app's choice, not a fault in your phone. Try a different app.
  • Reading tiny text and straining your eyes. Two apps on one screen means smaller words. Make the text bigger first, or give the app you are reading more room by dragging the divider. If the words are still too small, close the split and use one app at a time. One app at a time is always a fine choice.
  • Downloading an app to get split screen. You do not need one. Split screen and pop-up view are built into your Galaxy phone already. If a message tells you to install an app for split screen, ignore it. If you are unsure whether a message is real, read How to Spot a Scam Call or Message on Your Galaxy before you tap anything.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use split screen with any two apps?

Most apps work, but not all of them. Apps that need the whole screen, such as some games, may not offer split screen. The menu choice will be missing, or pale and untappable. Try the messaging app, the Phone app, the browser, or the Gallery first. If an app refuses, it is not a fault in your phone.

Does split screen use more battery?

Running two apps at once can use a little more battery, because the phone is doing two jobs. For a few minutes, the difference is small. If you leave two apps open in split screen for a long time, especially a video, the battery will go down faster. Close the split when you are done. For more ways to make your battery last, read How to Make Your Galaxy Battery Last Longer (simple settings).

Can I watch a video and write a message at the same time?

Yes, that is one of the best uses for split screen. Put the video on the top half and the messaging app on the bottom half. Tap the bottom half before you type, so the messaging app is active. If the video pauses when you tap the message half, tap the video half again and tap play. Some video apps pause when they are not the active half. That is normal.

How do I get back to one full-screen app?

Drag the divider bar all the way to the top edge or the bottom edge of the screen. The app on the side you drag towards will close, and the other app will fill the whole screen. Or tap the divider bar once and look for a close mark in the options that appear.

What is the difference between split screen and pop-up view?

Split screen divides the screen into two halves, top and bottom. Pop-up view keeps one app big and full screen, and puts the second app in a small floating window on top. Use split screen when both apps need room for a while. Use pop-up view when one app is only a quick job and the other should stay big.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words may look a little different on your phone. That is normal. Samsung makes many Galaxy models. Some are A-series phones. Some are S-series phones. They do not all show the exact same words. Your phone company, called a carrier, can also change some words. Your country or region can change words too. And your One UI version matters. In October 2026, some phones use One UI 8, some use One UI 8.5, and some use One UI 9, depending on the model and the region. No one version is "the" version for every phone. On your phone, the menu may say "Open in split screen view". On another phone, it may say something shorter, about split screen. Both mean the same choice. Look for the words "split screen" and "pop-up view" in the menu, even if the rest of the sentence is different.

If you cannot find the menu at all, go back to the Recents list and look again at the top edge of the app card. The small icon there is the key. Tap it slowly, and wait one second. If the menu still does not appear, check how you opened the Recents list. If your phone has buttons at the bottom, use the Recents button. If your phone uses swiping, swipe up from the bottom and hold your finger still in the middle of the screen. Using the wrong way to open Recents is a common reason the menu never appears. Trust the pictures and the key words: split screen, pop-up view, and the small X for closing. Words change. The ideas stay the same.
