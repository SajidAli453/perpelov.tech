How to Use Voice Typing on Your Galaxy Keyboard

Peter is 70 years old. His fingers ache. He has arthritis in his hands. Typing on a small phone keyboard hurts. He often makes mistakes. He deletes letters again and again. He gives up. He sends very short messages. His children think he is busy. He is not busy. He just finds typing hard. One day, his neighbour showed him something new. She showed him how to speak, and let the phone type the words. Peter was amazed. He spoke a message. The words appeared on the screen. He sent it. His daughter replied at once. Peter smiled. Typing did not hurt any more.

Voice typing means you speak and the phone turns your speech into text. You do not need to tap each letter. You tap a microphone icon on the keyboard. You speak clearly. The phone writes what you say. You can use voice typing for text messages. You can use it for notes. You can use it in many apps where the keyboard appears. It is not perfect. It may mishear a word. You can fix a word after. But it is much easier than typing every letter when your hands hurt, when your eyes are tired, or when you are in a hurry.

This guide explains voice typing in simple steps. It does not use hard words. It shows you what the microphone icon looks like. It shows you how to speak so the phone understands you. It shows you how to add a full stop. It shows you how to fix a mistake. You do not need to change many settings to try it. You only need a quiet place, a clear voice, and a little patience the first time. If it does not work well at first, try again. Speak a little slower. Speak a little louder. Move to a quieter room. Most people get better with a short practice.

Before you start: find the microphone icon

Open any app where you can type. For example, open your Messages app and start a new message to a family member. Tap the box where you would normally type. The keyboard will appear at the bottom of the screen. Look at the keyboard carefully. Look for a small icon that looks like a microphone. It is often near the bottom row of the keyboard. It may be near the space bar. It may be a small round button. On some keyboards, you may need to tap a small icon that looks like a keyboard or a set of dots to see more icons. Take your time. Look for the microphone shape. That is the voice typing button. If you cannot see it, do not worry. The last section of this guide explains what to look for and how to search for help with the wording on your phone.

Steps to use voice typing

  1. Open Messages or another app where you can type. Tap the message box. The keyboard will appear.
  2. Find the microphone icon on the keyboard. It looks like a small microphone. Tap it once. The keyboard may change. You may see a microphone screen. You may hear a short beep. The phone is now listening.
  3. Speak clearly and at a normal speed. Say one short sentence at a time. For example, say, Hello, I will visit on Sunday. Do not rush. Do not whisper. Hold the phone a short distance from your mouth. Speak as if you are talking to a person sitting next to you.
  4. Pause and look at the screen. The words you spoke should appear in the message box. Wait a second. The phone may still be thinking. Do not tap lots of buttons while you wait. Let the words appear.
  5. Say the punctuation if you want it. Say full stop for a period. Say comma for a comma. Say question mark for a question mark. Say new line if you want to start a new line. For example, say, I am fine full stop. The phone should write, I am fine. Practice this slowly. It feels strange at first. It soon feels normal.
  6. Tap the microphone icon again, or tap the keyboard icon if you see one, to stop listening. The phone will stop turning speech into text. You can now read what you spoke. Check for mistakes. Tap a wrong word. Delete it with the backspace key. Type the correct word, or speak that word again with voice typing.
  7. When the message looks right, tap Send. Your message will go to the other person. They will see normal typed text. They will not know you spoke it, unless you tell them. You did it. You sent a message without typing every letter.

Steps to speak so the phone understands you

  1. Find a quiet room. Turn off the television. Close a window if traffic is loud. Background noise makes voice typing harder. A quiet room gives the best result.
  2. Speak in short chunks. Say three to six words. Pause. Say the next chunk. Long, fast sentences are harder for the phone to understand. Short chunks are easier for you and for the phone.
  3. Use simple words. Say visit instead of a long, formal word. Say doctor instead of a long medical name if you can. Simple words are recognised more easily.
  4. Speak names slowly. Names of people and places can be hard for the phone. Say the name slowly and clearly. Check how the phone spells it. Fix it by hand if it is wrong. The phone learns from your voice over time, but names still need a check.
  5. If a word is wrong, do not start the whole message again. Fix only that word. Tap the word. Delete it. Speak it again, or type it. Fixing one word is faster and less tiring.

A small practice: Peter and the Sunday lunch message

Peter wanted to invite his son and daughter for Sunday lunch. In the past, he would type, Lunch Sunday? He would stop there. His hands hurt too much to type more. This time, he tried voice typing. He opened Messages. He tapped his daughter's name. He tapped the message box. He found the microphone icon near the bottom of the keyboard. He tapped it. The phone listened. Peter spoke slowly. He said, Hello, please come for lunch on Sunday, comma, at one o clock, full stop. He paused. The words appeared. He smiled. The phone had written almost every word correctly. It had misheard one word. It wrote lunch as launch. Peter tapped that word. He fixed it. He did not start again.

Peter tapped the microphone icon again. He spoke one more sentence. He said, I will make soup and bread, full stop. He stopped listening. He read the message. It looked good. He tapped Send. His daughter replied in a minute. She said, Thank you, Dad. We will be there. Peter felt proud. He had sent a full, warm message. His hands did not hurt. He now uses voice typing every day. He uses it for short messages. He uses it for longer messages too. He still checks each message before he sends it. He says the check is the most important step. You can practice the same way. Write a practice message to yourself or to a kind family member. Tell them you are practising. Speak slowly. Fix one word. Send it. That is enough for day one.

Common mistakes

  • Speaking too fast and in one long rush, so the phone misses words or joins them together, when short pauses between chunks would have made the text clear.
  • Forgetting to tap the microphone icon to stop listening, so the phone keeps typing background talk, television sounds, or a private conversation you did not mean to send.
  • Sending the message without reading it first, and sending a wrong word or a wrong name, when a ten second check would have caught the mistake.
  • Speaking in a noisy room with the television on, and blaming the phone when it mishears, when moving to a quiet room would have fixed most of the errors.
  • Giving up after one bad try on the first day, and going back to painful typing, when a second try with slower speech and shorter sentences often works much better.

Frequently asked questions

Does voice typing work without the internet?

It often works best with a connection. Some phones can do basic voice typing without a connection, but results may be less accurate. If voice typing is not working well, check that you are connected to Wi-Fi or mobile data. Try again in a place with a good signal.

Will other people know I used voice typing?

No. The other person sees normal text. They see the same words as if you had typed them. You can tell them if you want to. You do not have to tell them. The message looks the same.

Can I use voice typing in other languages?

Often, yes. The phone usually listens in the language of your keyboard. If you speak another language at home, look for the language setting on the keyboard or in the voice typing screen. Look for the word that means language, or for a globe icon. Choose your language there if you see it. If you cannot find it, ask a family member to help you look.

What if the microphone icon is missing from my keyboard?

Do not worry. The icon may be hidden behind another button. Look for a small icon that shows more options on the keyboard. Tap it and look for the microphone. Also check that voice typing is turned on. To check, look in your keyboard settings for words about voice input or voice typing. Look for a switch next to those words. If you cannot find the setting, use the search box in Settings and type voice typing. Ask a family member for help if the words still look different. Do not download a new keyboard app from a strange ad. Use the keyboard that came with your phone.

Is voice typing private? Who hears what I say?

Your speech is processed to turn it into text. It may be sent to a service to do that work. Do not dictate passwords, bank details, or other private codes with voice typing. Do not dictate private health details in a public place where others can hear you. For everyday messages to family, voice typing is a normal, useful tool. For secrets and codes, type them by hand, in private.

If the words on your screen look different

Menu words may differ by model, region, carrier, and One UI version. One UI 8, 8.5, and 9 all exist in October 2026. The keyboard on your phone may look a little different from the keyboard on a friend's phone. The microphone icon may be in a different corner. The words for voice input may be worded differently. Look for the microphone shape and for words like voice, voice input, or voice typing. Do not guess a long settings path from memory. Use the search box at the top of Settings and type voice typing. Tap the result that matches. If the screen still looks different, ask a family member to sit with you and look together. The microphone shape is your best guide. When you find it once, you will find it quickly next time.
